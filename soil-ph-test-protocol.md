# Soil pH Test Protocol — Apera PH20 Slurry Method

> Standard operating procedure for measuring soil pH with the Apera PH20 pH pen.
> Method: 1:1 soil:distilled-water slurry by weight, stir 1 min, stand 30 min.
> Context: companion to `soil-ph.md` (why pH matters in organic systems) and
> `cilantro-coriander-fertilizer.md` (pH 6.0–7.0 target).

---

## 1. Scope & basis

This protocol measures **water pH** on a 1:1 (weight) soil:distilled-water slurry.
It is the most common field/lab method (USDA NRCS SSIR-51 method 4.3.1.1.1.3;
UC Davis Western Region S 2.20; EPA Method 9045D; UF/IFAS SS665; NY DOT GTM-24b).

Note the dilution effect: 1:1 water pH reads ~0.15–0.25 units **higher** than
saturated-paste pH. Keep the ratio and settle time identical across samples so
readings are comparable to each other.

---

## 2. Equipment

- Apera PH20 pH pen (calibrated, see §5)
- pH 7.00 and pH 4.00 calibration buffers
- Clean **glass or polypropylene (plastic) beaker/jar**, ~100 mL — one per sample (or wash thoroughly between)
- **Distilled (or deionized) water** — for slurry AND for rinsing between samples
- Balance/scale (0.1 g resolution) or, if unavailable, a consistent scoop
- Stirring rod (glass or plastic)
- Timer
- Squirt bottle of distilled water + clean tissue (for rinsing/dabbing the probe)

---

## 3. Sample preparation

1. **Air-dry** each soil sample (spread thin, room temperature, out of direct sun, 1–2 days). Do not oven-dry.
2. **Remove** stones, roots, mulch, and plant debris. Break up clods. (Sieving to 2 mm is ideal, not required for a home reading.)
3. Use a clean stainless-steel tool. Avoid bare hands on the soil (salts/skin oils shift the reading).

---

## 4. Slurry procedure (per sample)

1. **Weigh 30 g** air-dried soil into a clean, labeled container.
2. **Add 30 g distilled water** (1:1 by weight; 30 mL = 30 g, so measuring mL is equivalent).
3. **Stir continuously for 1 minute** to fully suspend the soil.
4. **Let stand 30 minutes** for the suspension to settle. (Stir again briefly just before measuring.)
5. Repeat steps 1–4 for each additional sample, using the same ratio and settle time.

---

## 5. Calibration

1. Calibrate **once** at the start of the session — **not** between samples.
2. Two-point calibration: **pH 7.00 first**, then **pH 4.00**.
3. Rinse the probe with distilled water and shake/dab off excess between buffers.
4. Match buffer temperature to sample temperature where practical (the PH20 has automatic temperature compensation).
5. **Verify** after the session by dipping in pH 7.00 buffer — if it reads ~7.00, the session is valid. If off, recalibrate and redo.

---

## 6. Measurement

1. Stir the settled slurry gently.
2. Immerse the probe into the **slurry/supernatant** — do **not** jam the glass bulb into the settled sediment. Keep the tip ~2.5 cm (1 inch) below the surface, in the clearer liquid.
3. Stir gently and hold still. Wait for the smiley-face to appear **and stay solid** (reading stabilized).
4. Record pH to the **nearest 0.1 unit**.
5. Keep probe placement consistent across samples (position in the slurry affects the reading — "suspension effect").

---

## 7. Between-sample rinsing (IMPORTANT)

- **Rinse the probe with distilled (or deionized) water between every sample.**
- Do **not** use tap water for between-sample rinsing: dissolved tap-water ions can contaminate the next low-ion sample and shift its reading.
- Shake off excess water and dab gently with clean tissue before the next sample.
- **Tap water is acceptable only for final cleanup after all samples are done.**

---

## 8. Probe cleaning

### 8.1 Routine cleaning (after every session)

1. Rinse the probe thoroughly with **distilled water** to remove all soil particles. This is usually all that's needed.
2. Dab dry gently with clean tissue. **Do not rub the glass bulb** with your finger or anything hard — the bulb is fragile, and friction/static can cause measurement errors (Apera manual §9.3).

### 8.2 Tough contaminants (only if soil is caked on)

1. Detach the sensor shield.
2. Soak the probe in **cleaning solution (Apera AI1166) or mild detergent water** for 30 minutes.
3. Use a **soft brush** (e.g., soft toothbrush) with that water/detergent to remove residue — **not** dry-brushing, and go easy on the bulb.
4. **Then** soak in **3M KCl (AI1107) for at least 1 hour** — KCl *reconditions/rehydrates* the junction after cleaning; it is **not** the scrubbing step.
5. Rinse off, then **recalibrate** before next use.

> **3M KCl is a soaking/storage solution, not a cleaner.** Never brush *with* KCl.
> Brushing is done with water (or detergent water) + soft brush. KCl's only roles:
> reconditioning after a detergent clean, and storage (below).

## 9. Storage

1. **Never store the probe in pure water** (distilled, tap, RO, or deionized) — it leaches ions from the junction and permanently damages it. Pure water is for rinsing only.
2. **Short-term** (daily/weekly): keep the cap damp with a few drops of water or pH 4 buffer.
3. **Long-term** (>1 month): fill the cap **to the "Fill line"** with **3M KCl soaking solution (Apera AI1107)**, seal the red O-ring. A few drops is not enough — it evaporates and the probe dries out.
4. White crystals in/around the cap are normal KCl crystallization — rinse off, recalibrate before next use.

---

## 10. Consistency rules (what actually makes the numbers meaningful)

- Same **soil:water ratio** (1:1 by weight) for every sample.
- Same **mass** (30 g) and same **settle time** (30 min) for every sample.
- Same **water type** (distilled) for slurry and rinsing.
- Same **electrode placement** in the slurry for every reading.
- Air-dried (not field-moist) soil so the ratio is reproducible.
