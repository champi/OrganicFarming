# Otoe (*Xanthosoma*) Planting Material: Where to Get It Near Volcán

> **Web search, Nov 2026.** Where to get ~330 otoe "seeds" (corm pieces or cormels) for 150 m² at 1 m × 0.5 m. How much material, and what size: [otoe-yield-per-plant-spacing.md](otoe-yield-per-plant-spacing.md). How to pre-sprout it: [otoe-high-yield-organic-volcan.md](otoe-high-yield-organic-volcan.md).

## Short answer

- **No shop sells otoe "seed" online in Panama.** I found no listings on Encuentra24, on Facebook Marketplace, or at Volcán nurseries. Planting material moves through MIDA and IDIAP programs, through farmers, and through food markets.
- **Best lead: MIDA's seed validation center** at Potrerillos Arriba, Dolega (finca 170), about 1 h from Volcán. It validates otoe and gives *cepas* (mother corms) to small farmers and family gardens that ask. The head of the MIDA Potrerillos agency is Franklin Concepción (2024–2025 reports).
- **Cheapest bulk: mother corms from a farmer.** Markets sell the cormels and the farmer keeps or discards the mother corm (*cepa*), which is the best planting material (150–200 g pieces).
- **Fallback: small cormels from a food market**, planted whole. Prices are high now: otoe was over **$150/qq** wholesale in Aug 2026 (normally ~$0.70–0.80/lb).
- **Disease risk:** MIDA regulates moving otoe between provinces because of ***mal seco*** (*Pythium myriotylum*). It spreads in infected planting material and can cause 90–100% losses. Buy only firm, clean material from fields without yellowing or stunted plants.

**Not recommended:** bringing *tiquisque* from Costa Rica. It needs a MIDA import permit and post-entry quarantine, and Nicaragua brought in *mal seco* exactly this way, with Costa Rican planting material in the 1980s.

## Steps

1. Call or visit the MIDA Volcán or Bugaba agency. Ask for otoe *cepas* from the Potrerillos center, or for names of growers harvesting soon.
2. If MIDA has none, buy *cepas* from a grower at harvest. Top up with small cormels from Merca David.
3. Choose firm, heavy pieces with no soft spots, smell or dark streaks. Cut, cure, and pre-sprout them so only live, healthy pieces go into the soil.
4. Plant at the start of the rains (late Apr–May). If you get material earlier, pre-sprouting holds it.
