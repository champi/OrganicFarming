## Why Cherry Tomatoes?

Wild ancestor of all domesticated tomatoes is *Solanum pimpinellifolium* — a tiny, pea-sized fruit native to western South America (Perú, Ecuador). Cherry tomatoes are closer to the wild gene pool than large-fruited types, carrying natural resistance traits partially lost during selection for size. They tend to be:

- **More disease-resistant** (closer to wild genetics)
- **More vigorous** under stress (heat, irregular watering)
- **More prolific** — if some fruit is lost to pests, the plant still produces abundantly

## Primary Disease Threats in Volcán (Tropical Highland, ~1,400m)

| Disease | Pathogen | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| **Late Blight** | *Phytophthora infestans* | 15–25°C + wet foliage — the #1 killer |
| **Early Blight** | *Alternaria solani* | Humidity-driven, soil splash |
| **Fusarium Wilt** | *Fusarium oxysporum* | Warm, soil-borne |
| **Bacterial Wilt** | *Ralstonia solanacearum* | Tropical-specific, warm/wet soils |
| **Bacterial spot/speck** | *Xanthomonas/Pseudomonas* | High humidity |

### Disease Resistance Key

- **F** = Fusarium Wilt | **LB** = Late Blight | **EB** = Early Blight | **V** = Verticillium Wilt | **TMV** = Tobacco Mosaic Virus

### Why Sun Sugar, Not Sungold?

Sungold is the undisputed flavor champion (Cornell: 4.7/5 taste, Brix 9.3) but splits severely after rain. **Sun Sugar** is genetically similar with significantly better crack resistance — critical in Volcán's wet season. Multiple side-by-side growers prefer Sun Sugar's flavor (sweetness balanced with subtle acidity). Bred by Seminis; originally developed by the same team as Sungold.

### Why Matt's Wild Cherry?

In 2009 when late blight devastated the entire Northeastern US tomato crop, multiple Cornell reviewers reported Matt's Wild Cherry was **the only variety in their garden that survived** — *"planted in the midst of the carnage, they showed no signs of blight and produced until frost."* Collected from wild plants in Hidalgo, México (*S. lycopersicum* var. *cerasiforme*). Plant at field margins as biological insurance.

## Growing Recommendations for Volcán

### Dry Season (Dec–Apr): Plant Outdoors
- All 8 varieties thrive outdoors — no cover needed
- Trellis heavily (all are indeterminate, 6–8+ ft vines)
- Drip irrigation only — never overhead
- Heavy mulch (rice husk is perfect)
- Space 3 ft apart for airflow
- Prune lower 12–18" of foliage aggressively

### Wet Season (May–Nov): Use Rain Cover
- Even resistant varieties need a roof in Volcán's wet season
- Low tunnel with plastic roof, permanently open sides
- See [low-tunnel-construction-volcan.md] for materials and build guide
- Copper sprays preventatively during peak wet months (Jul–Sep)
