# Best Pumpkin Varieties — Organic, Humid-Tropical Highland

> **Volcán, Chiriquí, Panama. ~1,400m elevation. 16–24°C, 4,400mm annual rainfall, 74–94% humidity. Cfb climate.**

---

## Climate Fit: Yes, With the Right Species

Pumpkins need warm conditions. At Volcán's elevation (1,400m), the only viable species is **Cucurbita moschata** — tropical pumpkin, ayote, calabaza. It grows well up to 1,800m and thrives at daytime temps ≥20°C and nighttime >14°C. Volcán's daytime highs of 22–24°C and nighttime lows of 13–16°C are at the cool end of the range, but within it.

C. maxima and C. pepo (jack-o'-lantern types) are temperate-adapted and generally fail under tropical humidity.

**Key constraint:** In wet season (May–Nov, 500–630mm/month, 90–94% humidity), disease pressure is extreme. Downy mildew is the #1 threat — it thrives in cool, humid conditions. Dry season (Jan–Mar, 60–90mm/month, 74–80% humidity) is the best growing window.

## Tier 1 — Best for Volcán

### Soler 🇵🇷
The industry-standard tropical pumpkin of Puerto Rico and the Caribbean. Widely adapted across soil types (Oxisols, Vertisols, Ultisols). Yields 17,000–45,000 kg/ha. Moderate resistance to ZYMV — carries a recessive gene that reduces symptom severity. Thin skin doesn't need peeling for cooking. Can be planted in rainy season with drip irrigation. Tolerant of high silverleaf whitefly pressure. This is the most proven, widely available C. moschata cultivar for humid tropical conditions.

### South Anna Butternut 🇺🇸
A downy mildew-resistant butternut bred by Edmund Frost (Common Wealth Seed Growers, Virginia) from a cross of Seminole Pumpkin (Florida) × Waltham Butternut. The breakthrough variety for humid climates — keeps butternut shape and eating quality while inheriting Seminole's exceptional downy mildew resistance. Selected and improved annually since 2017. Named after the South Anna River in Virginia.

### Chinese Tropical Pumpkin 🇨🇳
Edmund Frost's highest-yielding C. moschata. Flattened, ribbed pumpkin shape similar to Fairytale. In SARE trials: yields over 70 lbs per plant in top selections, excellent downy mildew resistance, and outstanding eating quality (median Brix >11.5, dry matter >18%, flavor described as hazelnut). The best combination of yield + disease resistance + flavor in the trials.

### Taina Dorada 🇵🇷
Released 2012 by UPR-Mayagüez. Semi-bush habit (compact early, vining later) — the best candidate for tunnel growing. Superior eating quality: dry matter 11.7% (nearly 3× Soler's 4.1%), Brix 10.75 (2× Soler). Described as "pudding-like" texture. Smaller fruits (2–4 kg, 5–6 per 23kg sack). The semi-bush habit means it fits in tighter spaces and could work under a tunnel.

## Tier 2 — Specialty & Disease-Resistant

### Guatemalan Green Ayote 🇬🇹
The most unusual squash on this list. Some fruits are **green on the inside** — the color deepens in storage and cooking. Exceptional eating quality: very high dry matter and sugar, with flavor notes of spinach, candied papaya, and caramel. Exceptional downy mildew resistance. Excellent keeping quality (months of storage). Currently being selected for earliness and productivity by Common Wealth Seed Growers.

### Seminole Pumpkin 🇺🇸
A wild-type C. moschata from Florida, cultivated by the Seminole people. Virtually immune to downy mildew — the gold standard source of DM resistance in squash breeding. Extremely vigorous, survives neglect, insect-resistant. The fruit is variable: some excellent, some watery. Used primarily as the resistance donor in South Anna and other breeding lines. If you want something that will survive no matter what, this is it.

## Tier 3 — Virus-Resistant (Breeding Lines)

These varieties carry resistance to the potyviruses (PRSV, ZYMV) that devastate cucurbits in the tropics. They are primarily breeding materials — horticultural quality is variable — but they're worth knowing about for future crosses or if virus pressure is catastrophic.

### Menina 🇧🇷🇵🇹
Complete resistance to both PRSV and ZYMV. In Puerto Rico field trials: yield was **completely unaffected** by inoculation with either virus (vs. 100% yield loss in susceptible Waltham). Resistance is conferred by a single dominant gene (Zym) for ZYMV. The best source of potyvirus resistance for C. moschata.

### Nigerian Local 🇳🇬
The most famous virus-resistance source in Cucurbita breeding. Carries resistance to ZYMV (genes Zym-0 and Zym-4), PRSV (gene prv), WMV (gene Wmv), and CMV (gene Cmv). Used as the resistance donor in virtually all commercial virus-resistant squash. However: poor horticultural quality — irregular fruits, large seed cavities, low dry matter. Best used as a breeding parent, not for direct consumption.

### AVPU1426 🇧🇩
Developed by the World Vegetable Center (Taiwan) from a Bangladeshi landrace. Resistant to three begomoviruses (SLCCNV, ToLCNDV, SLCPV-TW) — the whitefly-transmitted viruses that cause leaf curl. Flat round, mottled green immature fruits with yellow flesh. Good yield (20.7 t/ha) and β-carotene (1.57 mg/100g). Resistance is recessive — needs homozygosity to express.

## Growing Strategy for Volcán

### Option A: Dry-Season Outdoor (Recommended)
Plant **Dec–Jan**, harvest **Mar–Apr**. Best varieties: Soler, South Anna, Chinese Tropical Pumpkin. Lower humidity (74–80%), lower aphid pressure, less disease. Drip irrigation recommended. Use varieties that DON'T need virus resistance since vector pressure is low.

### Option B: Wet-Season Tunnel
Plant **May–Jun**, harvest **Aug–Oct**. Only viable for 1–2 plants per 3×6m module due to space. Best varieties: **Taina Dorada** (semi-bush habit fits tunnels), **South Anna** (downy mildew resistance as insurance even under cover). **Must hand-pollinate** — 40-mesh insect net blocks bees. Tunnel provides rain protection which dramatically reduces foliar disease, but the tunnel is designed to stay cool (≤+2°C above ambient), so it won't help with temperature.

### Variety Combinations for 3-Plant Starter Set

**All-purpose humid tropics starter:**
1. Soler — proven Caribbean workhorse, ZYMV resistance
2. South Anna Butternut — DM-resistant, great eating quality, different shape/use
3. Chinese Tropical Pumpkin — max yield + DM resistance + dessert quality

**Tunnel-optimized starter:**
1. Taina Dorada — semi-bush habit, superior flavor, fits under cover
2. South Anna Butternut — DM resistance insurance
3. Chinese Tropical Pumpkin — high yield under cover

**Maximum disease resistance (for wet-season gamble):**
1. Seminole Pumpkin — will survive anything
2. South Anna Butternut — bred for the humid Southeast
3. Guatemalan Green Ayote — exceptional DM resistance + best keeping quality
