# Other Squash for Volcán — Beyond Pumpkin

> **Volcán, Chiriquí, Panama. ~1,400m elevation. 16–24°C, 4,400mm annual rainfall, 74–94% humidity.**

---

## 1. Chayote (Sechium edule) — The #1 Perfect Fit

**This is the single best squash for Volcán. Period.** It's native to exactly this climate — the cloud forests of southern Mexico and Central America at 500–1,700m. It's already grown commercially across Costa Rica and Panama.

| Volcán Condition | Chayote Requirement | Match? |
|---|---|---|
| 1,400m elevation | 300–2,000m (optimum) | ✅ Perfect |
| 16–24°C | 15–28°C (optimum 25°C daytime, <23°C night) | ✅ Perfect |
| 74–94% humidity | 80–85% required | ✅ Perfect |
| 4,400mm rainfall | 1,500–2,000mm minimum | ✅ More than enough |
| ~12h day length | 12–12.5h required for flowering | ✅ Perfect |

**What you get:**
- Perennial vine to 12m — plant once, harvest for years
- 80–100 fruits per plant per season
- Every part edible: immature fruit (main crop), young leaves and shoot tips (cooked greens), tuberous roots (starch — like a potato), and mature fruit
- Fruits keep 2–4 weeks unrefrigerated
- Grown on trellises or over trees/structures
- One single large seed per fruit — plant the whole fruit to propagate
- Commercial yields of 40–60 t/ha in Costa Rica

**Varietal diversity:** At least 12 varietal groups exist in Mexico alone, plus 25+ landraces across Central America. The two main commercial types:
- **Virens levis** — medium, pale green, smooth, pear-shaped (most common export type)
- **Nigrum spinosum** — dark green, spiny (second most traded)
- **Albus dulcis** — white/yellow, sweetest, best for eating raw or in desserts
- Local landraces throughout Panama and Costa Rica

**Disease concerns:** Powdery mildew and anthracnose are the main diseases. Nematodes and cucumber beetles are key pests. Much less disease pressure than Cucurbita pumpkins — chayote co-evolved with Central American humidity.

**Seed source:** You don't buy seeds — you buy a chayote fruit from any local market in Chiriquí and plant it. The fruit IS the seed. Plant the whole fruit at a 45° angle, shoot end exposed. Start at the beginning of the rainy season. You're in the center of chayote diversity.

## 2. Fig-Leaf Gourd / Chilacayote (Cucurbita ficifolia) — The Highland Specialist

C. ficifolia is the most cold-tolerant of all cultivated Cucurbita species — it grows at **1,000–3,000m** in the Andes and Mexican highlands. Volcán's 1,400m is the low end of its range. It actually *prefers* cool, humid conditions. While other Cucurbita species struggle and succumb to disease in Volcán's wet season, C. ficifolia thrives.

| Trait | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Climate** | Cool, humid highlands 1,000–3,000m. Maintains vigor when other Cucurbita perish. |
| **Temperature** | Cold-tolerant. Killed only by frost (not an issue in Volcán). |
| **Day length** | Short-day for flowering (12h day triggers it — matches Volcán year-round) |
| **Disease** | Resistant to several viruses, tolerates powdery mildew. The hardiest Cucurbita. |
| **Yield** | Massive vines to 25m. Very productive. |
| **Storage** | Mature fruits last **2–5 YEARS** unrefrigerated — unmatched in the plant kingdom for squash. |

**What you eat:**
- **Young fruits** (tennis ball size) — cooked as summer squash
- **Mature flesh** — candied with sugar (traditional Latin American "dulce de chilacayote" / "cabellos de ángel" — angel hair jam). This is the #1 use across Latin America.
- **Male flowers** — in soups, stews, salads
- **Young leaves and shoot tips** — cooked greens
- **Seeds** — roasted, high protein snack

**Names across the world:**
- Chilacayote / chiverre (Mexico, Central America, Colombia)
- Fig-leaf gourd / Malabar gourd (English)
- Shark fin melon (China — flesh used as shark fin substitute)
- Kahurura (Kenya)
- Asian pumpkin / Thai marrow (SE Asia)
- Grown commercially in Philippines highlands at 1,700m

**Growing notes:** It's a short-day plant — flowers when daylight drops below ~12.5 hours. At Volcán's latitude (~8.7°N), day length is nearly constant year-round at ~12 hours, which means it should flower continuously. Plant at the start of the rainy season. Trellis strongly — vines reach 15–25m.

**Where to find it:** May already exist in Chiriquí highlands — it's common in Costa Rica and Colombian highlands. Also available as "Chilacayote" from some US seed companies. Experimental Farm Network offers a northern-adapted strain. In Panama, check with IDIAP or local highland farmers.

## 3. C. moschata Summer Squash — Vine Borer-Immune Zucchini Alternative

Most summer squash (zucchini, yellow squash, pattypan) is Cucurbita pepo — and it gets destroyed by humidity diseases. But summer squash from the **Cucurbita moschata** species is naturally immune to vine borers (Melittia satyriniformis) and highly resistant to plectosporium blight — two of the biggest cucurbit killers in humid climates.

These are eaten young and tender — same as zucchini — but the plants are vining (not bush) and take a bit longer to start producing. Once they start, they keep going until frost.

## 4. Tatume (C. pepo) — Mexican Survivor Squash

The most disease-resistant C. pepo variety tested in SARE trials. Survived Plectosporium blight when ALL other pepo varieties (zucchini, yellow squash, pattypan) died. Vining habit (not bush). Round, light green fruits harvested at tennis ball size as summer squash. If left to mature, turns streaked orange and can be used as winter squash. Drought-tolerant — evolved in arid Mexico. Thin, delicate vines that root at nodes.

**Disease profile in SARE trials:**
- Plectosporium blight: **Resistant** (only C. pepo variety that survived)
- Downy mildew: **Moderate resistance** (produced well into late season, eventually succumbed)
- Anasa wilt: Good resistance

**Seed source:** Widely available from US seed companies (Baker Creek, Southern Exposure, Johnny's, etc.). Much easier to find than the moschata summer squash.

## 5. Philippine C. moschata Varieties — Bred for Your Conditions

Beyond the Suprema/Bella F1 hybrids already covered in the pumpkin doc, several older Philippine varieties offer disease resistance:

#### Rizalina 🇵🇭
OP variety from UPLB (University of the Philippines Los Baños). Large yellow-orange fruits, deeply ridged, thick flesh, 4.2–5.0kg. **Tolerant to squash virus complex.** Good for baking and cooking. 57–80 days to flowering depending on season. Yields 26–38 t/ha.

#### Victoria F1 🇵🇭 (Kaneko Seeds)
73 days. Mature kalabasa (3–4 kg, ridged, green with white spots), not a summer squash. **Tolerant to mosaic virus, powdery AND downy mildew** (vendor claim). High yielding, easy fruit set.

#### Gloria F1 🇵🇭 (Kaneko Seeds)
82 days. **High tolerance to virus, powdery AND downy mildew.** High yielding. Triple disease resistance in one hybrid.

## Summary: What to Grow in Volcán (Ranked — literature review, Oct 2026)

**Volcán's main Cucurbita killer is downy mildew.** DM thrives at 10–24°C with leaf-surface RH >85% (UConn IPM), which is Volcán's climate almost year-round. That makes trial-measured DM resistance the main climate criterion. Second is temperature: in Mexico, C. moschata is mostly grown below 1,000 m (optimum ~20–30°C), C. pepo above 1,000 m, and C. ficifolia above 1,300 m (Villanueva 2007, via Ríos-Santos et al.; Valdivia-Bernal et al. 2020). At 1,400 m and 16–24°C, Volcán sits at the cool edge of moschata's range and in the core range of pepo, ficifolia and chayote.

Flavor scores come from the SARE FS23-347 trials (Virginia/Tennessee 2023). Care of the Earth rated flavor 1–5 with **1 = best**. Twin Oaks rated sweetness with higher = sweeter. "—" means the variety was not rated.

### The Chayote Reality

Chayote is so obvious for Volcán that it almost doesn't need a research doc. You're in its center of origin (Mesoamerica to Panama). Costa Rica, your neighbor, is the world's leading exporter. The fruit IS the seed. Walk to any market in Volcán or David, buy a chayote, and plant it. One plant on a trellis feeds a family for years.

The research literature confirms what geography already tells you: *"In the Indonesian highlands it is, in quantity, the most important vegetable produced. In Java it is abundant in the highlands between 700 and 1,800m."* — PROSEA. Same elevation range as Volcán. Same result.
