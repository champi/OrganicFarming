# Bone Ash (P) and SOP (K) Doses for 8 Garden Crops — Volcán Andisol

> **Literature review, Oct 2026.** Per-plant amounts, timing and placement of raw bone ash (phosphorus) and potassium sulfate (SOP, potassium) for pumpkin, climbing bean, corn, papaya, potted lemon, otoe, sweet potato, cherry tomato and strawberry, with mycorrhizae (AMF) taken into account. Background: [bone-ash-phosphorus-fertilizer.md](bone-ash-phosphorus-fertilizer.md) (§11 AMF, §15 dose rule, §16) and [zucchini-nutrition-guide.md](zucchini-nutrition-guide.md) (§3 P, §4 K).

**Assumptions:** medium soil P and K (no soil test yet), plants inoculated with or growing near AMF, ash at ~16% P, SOP at 50% K₂O. Zucchini is covered in its own trial sheet: 15 g ash once, 3 × 5 g SOP.

## 2. Method

### Bone ash (P)
- **Raw, fine-ground ash** (<250 µm, flour-like), mixed **1:1 by weight with vermicast**.
- **Mix it into a ring of soil**, not a pocket. Pull the mulch back, scratch a trench 5–8 cm deep at the ring distance, sprinkle evenly, stir it through ~1–2 L of soil, re-cover and water.
- **No citric acid needed.** The AMF and their bacteria dissolve the apatite, and slow P doesn't switch the symbiosis off (bone-ash guide §11).
- **Dose rule:** ~2–3× the crop's P uptake (bone-ash guide §15). Lower it for strongly mycorrhizal crops.
- **Repeat crops in the same bed:** about half the dose.

### SOP (K)
- **Apply it dry, straight from the bag.** No need to dissolve it.
  - Dry SOP spread on the soil is the standard field method.
  - In blueberry, one dry spring application of SOP matched weekly dissolved SOP through drip in yield, fruit quality and leaf K [K4].
  - SOP's salt index is low: 42.6, against 116 for potassium chloride (KCl) [K1, K2].
- **Spread it thinly along the same ring and scratch it into the top 1–2 cm.**
  - Keep it 5–10 cm from stems and off wet leaves.
  - Never put it in the seed hole: keep under ~10 lb/acre of N + K₂O in the seed row [K2].
  - Water it in.
- **Split it into small doses.** Volcanic (allophanic) soils have no K-fixing clays, and ~25% of applied K leached [K5]. On high-rainfall volcanic soils, K is split 2–4 times a year [K6].
- **Measuring:** weigh 5 g once to calibrate a spoon. A level teaspoon is roughly 5–8 g depending on how fine the powder is.
- **Rate logic:** as in the zucchini guide, SOP covers ~60–70% of crop K uptake. Urine, vermicast and soil supply the rest.
- **Compatibility:** same day as urine is OK; keep ≥3 days from wood ash.

## 3b. Literature check of the gram doses (Nov 2026)

Rule used throughout: bone ash ≈ 2–3× the crop's P uptake (less with AMF); SOP ≈ 60–70% of K uptake (urine, vermicast and soil supply the rest). Vermicast's own P and K are negligible at these weights (≈0.2–0.4% P, 0.4–0.9% K dry [C9]); it is the carrier and microbe source, not the nutrient source.

| Crop | Uptake per plant (literature) | Ash given → P | × uptake | SOP given → K | % of K uptake | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Climbing bean | P 0.07–0.15 g; K 0.5–1 g (bush beans, 12–18 kg P/ha in Rwanda climbing beans) [P5, C4, C5] | 2.5 g → 0.4 g P | 3–5× | 1 g → 0.4 g K | 40–80% | OK |
| Corn | P 0.4–0.5 g; K ~1.5 g [P1, K7] | 6 g/stalk → 1 g P | 2–2.5× | 2.5 g/stalk → 1 g K | ~70% | OK |
| Pumpkin | Tetsukabuto aerial parts at 105 days: **P 4.7 g, K 26.7 g** [C1]; K is the nutrient taken up most [P6] | 50 g → 8 g P | 1.7× | old 30 g → 12.5 g K | **47%** | **SOP low → raised to 45 g (3 × 15 g ≈ 66%)** |
| Papaya | Year 1 aerial parts: **P 6.3 g, K 62.8 g** [C2]; Embrapa yr 1 ≈ 70 g P₂O₅, 120 g K₂O [P13]; India 250 g P₂O₅ + 300–500 g K₂O/yr for 186 t/ha [C3] | 150–200 g/yr → 24–32 g P | 4–5× | 240 g/yr → 100 g K | 160% of yr-1 uptake | OK; ×1.5 SOP once fruiting heavily |
| Lemon, 10 L pot | Citrus nursery tree in 3.8 L: optimum **P 2.9 g, K 8 g** per plant; P had little effect [C6] | 10 g → 1.6 g P | — | old 10 g/yr → 4 g K | ~50% of a nursery tree | **SOP raised to 20 g/yr (1 tsp every 3 months)** |
| Strawberry | P 0.2–0.35 g; K 1.5–5 g [S1–S4] | 5 g → 0.8 g P | 2–4× | 6–8 g/yr → 2.5–3.3 g K | 65–100% | OK |

**Bone-ash effectiveness.** Bone char matched TSP for P uptake in AMF-inoculated maize on a P-fixing soil, but not without AMF [C7]. Bone meal's immediate effectiveness was ≥90% of fused phosphate, with P-use efficiency 12–14% (73–84% of the mineral source) [C8]. That low recovery is why the doses are 2–5× uptake.

**Comparison with conventional recs.** Brazilian (IAC) pumpkin recs are much higher: 200–400 kg P₂O₅ and 160–320 kg K₂O/ha, with K topdress optimum ~199 kg K₂O/ha [C10, C11]. They are written for P-poor Oxisols with soluble fertilizer, no AMF; use them only as an upper bound.

**Biggest unknown: soil K.** No published exchangeable-K data were found for Volcán; Cerro Punta farms tested high (210–567 mg/L, urine guide). A K test would decide whether the raised pumpkin/papaya SOP is needed or can be halved. Andisols leach K, so small splits beat single doses (split + K₂SO₄ beat single application in West Java Andisol potato) [K5, C12].

## 4. Caveats

- **Soil K may already be high.** Cerro Punta farms tested high in K (210–567 mg/L, IDIAP; urine guide). If your soil tests high, halve SOP or skip it.
- **Mg is typically high here.** Extra K competes with Mg and Ca for uptake, so don't over-apply.
- **One soil test** (Modified-Olsen P, exchangeable K/Ca/Mg) would replace the "medium" assumption for every row.
- **Otoe and potted-lemon rates are extrapolations.** There are no bone ash or SOP trials for either.
- **Stage matters.** A crop past its P window (sweet potato >6 wk, corn past the 6-leaf stage) gets a half dose or none.
