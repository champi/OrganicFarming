# Does uneven pollination make zucchini misshapen?

Literature review, 2026-11-25. Context: the President F1 trial at the rented house ([zucchini-trial-2026-president-f1.md](zucchini-trial-2026-president-f1.md)) gave 6 fruits of about 40 g, one of 150 g that looked "half pollinated", and one good fruit of 260 g. Only plant 4 made male flowers.

## Answer

- **Yes, along the length; probably not side to side.** In squash, too little pollen gives small fruit with a **pointed, thin blossom end**. The story you often read, where pollen on one stigma lobe makes one side of the fruit swell, comes from **watermelon**. It was tested in squash and did not hold.
- **The amount of pollen matters more than where it lands.** More viable pollen gives more seeds, faster growth, bigger fruit and fewer aborted fruit.

## Evidence

### Watermelon and cucumber: growth stays close to the seeds

- **Watermelon** (Mann 1943): pollen tubes mostly stay in the carpel under the stigma lobe they landed on; only about 21–22% cross into each neighbouring carpel. Uneven pollen across the 3 lobes made the carpels grow unequally, so the fruit was lopsided, mostly at the blossom end. Extension guides add "≥1,000 grains spread over all 3 lobes" (McGregor).
- **Cucumber**:
  - Fruit grows unevenly by sector because pollen tubes do not reach the ovules at the stem end. Pollen-tube contact alone, without fertilization, is enough to make the ovary grow (Varga & Bruinsma 1990).
  - In long cucumbers, pollen tubes never got past the half of the ovary nearest the flower (den Nijs & Milotay 1991).
  - Fruit tissue next to seeds enlarges more than the rest (Denna 1973). Parthenocarpic cucumbers that get partly pollinated turn misshapen.
- **How curving works**: a curved cucumber has 1.4–2× more auxin on its outer side. Putting auxin (NAA) on one side of a straight ovary made it curve, and putting it on the inner side of a curved one straightened it (Li et al. 2020).

### Squash: no side-to-side effect

- **Gustafson 1938** (pumpkin and crookneck squash): pollen was put on one side only, or after removing up to 5/6 of the stigma.
  - Seeds were still spread evenly around the fruit, and there were far fewer of them.
  - The fruit was still **normal in size and shape**.
- **Traub & O'Rork 1939** explain why: the pollen tubes enter the fruit cavity at its centre, at the blossom end. Which ovules get fertilized then depends on their **distance** from that entry point, not on which lobe the pollen landed on.
- Spanish gardening sites that say "pollen on one lobe → seeds only on that side" repeat the watermelon claim. They give no squash data.

### Squash: the effect runs along the length

- **Where the seeds are** (Nerson & Paris 2001): in well hand-pollinated zucchini, 42% of the seed weight is in the blossom-end third, 56% in the middle third and **2% in the stem-end third**. The stem end is seedless even with good pollination.
- **Poorly pollinated fruit** (Inagro/Craeye, Belgium): it stays small, turns **pointed and often dark at the tip** (*puntvrucht*), and goes soft and rots faster in storage. Fruit set "starts at the stem and moves toward the tip."
- **Male flower shortage**: this was the main cause in the Belgian work. Males go missing when the mean daily temperature stays below about 15 °C, and a male:female ratio below about 1:9 is too low (Craeye et al. 2020).
- **Pointed tip in practice**: Spanish grower guides list "thick at the base, thin or wrinkled at the tip" as the incomplete-pollination pattern. Many extension pages also list curved, bell-shaped and nubbin fruit.

### Pollen amount → seeds → size

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| A bigger pollen load gives more seeds per fruit, and fruits with more seeds are more likely to mature | Winsor et al. 1987 (zucchini) |
| Fruits with more seeds **grow faster and get bigger**; the fruit with the fewest seeds aborts first; a fruit already developing with many seeds suppresses later ones | Stephenson et al. 1988 (zucchini) |
| Fruit is bigger and has more seeds when pollinated on the day the flower opens; seed number correlates with fruit weight | Pacini & Nepi 2001 |
| No pollination: 56% still reached marketable size (Tosca), but shorter, lighter and thinner; hand ≈ open pollination | Knapp & Osborne 2017 |
| Full fruit set needs ~1,250–2,300 grains per stigma; a honey bee leaves 53–230 grains per visit | Vidal 2010; Stoner 2020 |
| Hand pollination with a whole anther gave as many seeds as bees; half an anther gave fewer | Brazilian squash seed study (Hortic. Bras.) |

### Counterpoints

- Pollen load changed seed numbers but **not the number of fruits** in 4 squash cultivars (Nerson 2009).
- In cucumber, Tiedjens (1928) found no link between seed number and fruit shape.
- In mature squash, a few seeds were enough for normal size and shape (Gustafson 1938). Immature zucchini picked at day 5–10 may react differently, and no study measures zucchini shape against pollen load directly.
- President has some parthenocarpy: 29% of bagged flowers set fruit (Robinson 1993, only 14 flowers). Some fruit grows with few or no seeds, and then the plant's own hormones set the shape, not the seeds.

### Pollen and timing

- Female flowers are open about 6 h. The stigma is receptive from 1 day before to 2 days after opening, but the **ovules only on the day before and the day of opening** (Nepi & Pacini 1993; Pacini & Nepi 2001).
- Pollen viability:
  - starts to drop 3 h after the flower opens
  - about 60% when the flower closes
  - about 10% the next morning
  - pollen stored 5 days or more set no marketable fruit, against 90% with fresh pollen (Craeye et al. 2018).
- Dry air kills squash pollen fast: 4 h at ≤80% relative humidity → ≥90% dead.

### Other causes of misshapen fruit (rule out)

- **Virus** (ZYMV, CMV, WMV): knobby, mottled fruit; mosaic or distorted leaves.
- **Potassium deficiency**: pinched stem end.
- **Uneven watering**: constrictions, pear shapes.
- **Physical obstacle**: curve or hook.
- **Developing fruit on the same plant**: later fruits grow slowly or stall (Stephenson 1988).

## What this means for the trial

- The results fit **pollen limitation**:
  - only one plant (the cat-damaged one) made males;
  - on Nov 18 the male flower used did not look healthy;
  - the rule of 1 male per 5 females spreads pollen thin.
- **To check**: cut a misshapen fruit lengthwise. Seeds only near the blossom end, with an empty, narrow tip, means short pollen.
- **Hand-pollination method**:
  1. Use a male that opened **the same morning**.
  2. Pollinate before about 9–10 am, while the flower is dry.
  3. Rub the anther over **every stigma lobe** until each is visibly yellow. Covering all lobes doesn't change the shape in squash, but it raises the total pollen load.
  4. While males are scarce and the fruit matters, use **1 male per 1–2 females**.
- Keep a male-heavy pollen plant (see [farm-rules-to-remember.md](farm-rules-to-remember.md)).
