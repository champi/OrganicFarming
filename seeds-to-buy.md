## Tithonia diversifolia (botón de oro / Mexican sunflower)

**Why:** a hedge plant to cut for green manure/compost (leaves ≈3.5% N, 0.37% P, 4% K dry weight; Jama et al. 2000) and as animal fodder (≈20–28% crude protein, feed as part of the diet only). It does **not** fix N. It scavenges nutrients, so put urine or ash back on the hedge row, and pair it with a legume hedge (Gliricidia/Inga).

**Seller:** AgroEnlace. Contact: Sr. Jorge (George Chong), **6044-5252**, agroenlace@gmail.com. [Listing](https://agroenlace.com.pa/anuncio/semillas-de-boton-de-oro/): "Semillas de Botón de Oro", $6.00 per dozen, ships to all of Panama. The listing has no date.

**Ask before paying:**
- Is "a dozen" **cuttings or seeds**? Prefer cuttings: Tithonia seed is unreliable (50–62% non-viable in one Colombian study).
- Cuttings should be **30–50 cm** long, from the **lower or middle, woody part of the stem**. In a Buga (Colombia) trial these rooted at 94% and 58%, against 6% for soft tips ([LRRD 6(3)](https://www.lrrd.org/lrrd6/3/9.htm)).
- How long in transit? Plant them within a few days.

**Free backups if AgroEnlace fails:**
- Roadside and fence-line plants around Volcán, Cerro Punta and Boquete. Look for yellow flowers in the dry season (about Nov–Feb).
- Local dairy or goat farmers with protein banks.
- IDIAP Chiriquí station or MIDA regional Chiriquí.

**Planting:**
- Plant cuttings the right way up (top end up), with about ⅓ buried.
- Spacing is about 0.5–1 m in a hedge.
- Cut every 6–8 weeks, at 60–80 cm height, before it flowers or sets seed (it can be invasive).
- Let fresh mulch sit about 2 weeks before sowing small seeds into it (it inhibits germination).
- Once 10–12 plants are established, take your own cuttings from them.
