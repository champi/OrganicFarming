# Peppers: Best-Tasting Varieties for Volcán (sweet, mild-flesh, heatless)

> Research compiled Nov 13–15, 2026 (/tasty-seed, /lit). Covers bell and other sweet peppers, mild peppers, hot peppers whose flesh is mild once the ribs and seeds are removed, heatless versions of hot peppers, and ají dulce. **Next step: pick what to buy** (see [Shortlist](#shortlist-to-choose-from)).
>
> Evidence types: **panel** = trained or consumer tasting, **chef** = chef panel or showcase, **lab** = measured sugar (Brix) or heat compound, **award**, **legend** = protected origin or heritage status, **anecdote** = forums, reviews, seller claims.

---

## 1. Key findings

1. **Species decides the growing setup in Volcán** (about 20 °C average, nights about 14–16 °C in the dry season, 74–94% humidity):

   | Species | Examples | Setup |
   |---|---|---|
   | *C. annuum* | Bell, Italian, kapia, Padrón, Manganji, jalapeño | **Rain shelter.** Cool nights at or below ~14 °C give flat, seedless, misshapen bells; long types cope better. |
   | *C. baccatum* | Ají amarillo | **Rain shelter.** Peruvian guides give a best average of 18–22 °C, day 20–25 °C, night 16–18 °C; above 28 °C hurts the fruit. Closest climate match of all. Second-best cold tolerance after rocoto (Ou 2014). |
   | *C. chinense* | Ají dulce, Habanada, Notta Hotta, Biquinho, panca | **Hot closed tunnel.** At a 19–20 °C average, flowering came a month later and yield fell about 85% (Jaimez & Rada 2015). In a UMass trial, the high tunnel beat the open field. |
   | *C. pubescens* | Rocoto | Fits the cool climate but is very hot (heat runs into the flesh), so it was dropped. |

2. **Pick fully ripe.** Ripening raised sugars about 87% on average across 13 pepper types (Guijarro-Real 2022). The year's weather moved sugar as much as the variety did, so any single-trial Brix ranking is a weak guide.
3. **"Hot seeds, mild flesh" is real for *C. annuum* and *C. baccatum*.** Green Padrón had 277 µg/g of heat compound in the ribs vs 2 µg/g in the flesh (Palma 2020). Ají amarillo and cerezo had the least heat compound in the flesh of the Peruvian ajíes tested (UPCH 2016). Superhots make the heat compound in the flesh too, so removing the ribs doesn't work for them (Tanaka 2021).
4. **What makes a pepper taste good** (Wageningen trained panel, Eggink): texture (juiciness, crunch) and the sweet-vs-sour balance matter most. Sweetness follows fructose and glucose; fruity aroma follows (E)-2-hexen-1-ol.
5. **Why Peru seemed absent:** Peru is famous for *exporting* sweet peppers (piquillo from Spain, imported paprika and bell varieties grown in the northern coastal desert), not for breeding them. Peru's own peppers are mostly *C. baccatum* and *C. chinense*.
6. **Cross-pollination:** keep heatless *C. chinense* (ají dulce, Habanada) away from hot habaneros, or the next generation turns hot. Korean crunchy peppers likewise turn hot when grown next to hot types.

## 3. Peru (literature summary)

- **Ají amarillo / escabeche** (*C. baccatum*): grown July–December on the cool, cloudy central coast (Huaral, Barranca). Yield is about 16 t/ha fresh, up to 30+ in trials. The best temperatures above match Volcán.
- **Ají panca:** mild, about 500–1,500 Scoville (INIA measured 1,500 fresh). Peru's seed bank (INIA) lists it as *C. chinense*, so it needs a hot tunnel and 149–189 days. Used dried, for berry and smoky color and flavor.
- **Amazon "ají dulce"** (*C. chinense*): UNALM calls it "the exception to the rule that every ají is hot." INIA's aromatic, high-yield Dulce Rojo and Tomatito Rojo are not sold.
- 12 of 50 native ajíes had almost no heat compound in the flesh (INIA/UPCH 2016). Aroma, not heat, is what sets Peruvian ajíes apart.
- No low-heat ají amarillo variety has been released; it is only a stated breeding goal.

## 4. Regional legends not currently buyable

- **Florina (Greece, protected origin):** highest sweetness index vs Karatzova (2021); thick-fleshed roasting pepper.
- **Pontecorvo (Italy, protected origin):** very thin skin, "highly digestible and sweet."
- **Other Spanish protected-origin peppers:** Piquillo de Lodosa, Gernika (heat compound 0.03 µg/g, essentially zero), Riojano, Bierzo, Fresno-Benavente, Arnoia, Oímbra, Couto, Mougán.
- **Italy:** Senise (dried, fried until crisp), Carmagnola and Quadrato d'Asti.
- **Korea:** "ŏi-gochu" / crunchy pepper, a mild, crisp, thick-walled green pepper (no Brix data).
- **Turkey:** Çarliston (sweet, thin-walled, for frying), Kandil dolma (stuffing).
- **South Africa:** Peppadew / Juanita (*C. baccatum*, about 1,000 Scoville, sweet-tangy when pickled); the branded line is patented.

## 5. Growing notes for Volcán

- **Structure:** rain shelter for *C. annuum* and *C. baccatum*; closed hot tunnel for *C. chinense*. See [volcan-tunnel-design.md](volcan-tunnel-design.md).
- **Cool nights:** at or below 14 °C, pollen dies and bells come out deformed. Shake the flowers to spread pollen; repeated pollination improves fruit shape (Pressman 1998). Long types (bull's horn, Nardello, Manganji) suffer less than blocky bells. Israeli breeding produced bells that set at 8–10 °C nights (Cannon, Melchor), but they're commercial and hard to find.
- **Disease** (rain and humidity): anthracnose, bacterial spot, *Phytophthora* root rot, soft rot. Keep leaves dry, ventilate, use raised beds.
- **Harvest fully ripe** for sugar; red is sweeter than green.
