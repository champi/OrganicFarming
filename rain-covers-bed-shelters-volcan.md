## Tunnel vs rain cover

| | Tunnel | Rain cover |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Walk-in structure (hoops or a gable frame) with a clear plastic roof and open or mesh sides. The farm model is a Benguet double-cladding gable with a ridge vent. | Clear plastic roof only, sides open. Either a low arch over one bed, or a taller open-sided shelter over one or more beds. |
| Cost | Higher | Low; can be temporary or movable |
| Protection | Most; suits long crops (tomato, cucumber) | Keeps rain off leaves and soil |
| Humidity | Needs venting | Lower, because the sides are open |

**Why rain cover works:** keeping rain off the leaves and soil means less disease and less soil splashed onto plants, less fruit cracking, and fewer nutrients washed out of the soil (Kratky 2006).

**Measured gains**

| Study | Setup | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Arya et al. 2001, Belize | Open-sided plastic shelters | Tomato +169 %, pepper +96 %, no pesticide, few weeds |
| Kratky 2006, Hawaii | 0.8 m-wide single-row rain covers, 0.7 m vent gap between them | Salable tomato +13–19 %, grade 1 +31–95 %, less cracking |
| PCAARRD / Benguet State University, Philippine highlands | Double-cladding shelter: clear plastic plus 40–50 % shade net | Broccoli +47 %, lettuce +22 %, strawberry +21 % |
| Bec Hellouin, France | Under cover vs outside | €80/m² vs €38–48/m² |

**Volcán caution:** keep the sides open or vented. A sealed cover in 85–95 % humidity brings mildew and slugs (see [zucchini-establishment-spacing-tunnel-volcan.md](zucchini-establishment-spacing-tunnel-volcan.md)).

## Why tunnel beds are wider (1.2–1.3 m)

- Covered space is the most expensive and most productive ground: frame and plastic cover every m², paths included. Each path you remove turns covered ground into growing ground.
- The tunnel's width is fixed, so you can only fit more bed into it. Dowding uses three paths and 1.3 m centre beds rather than four paths and narrower beds, with 30 cm paths.
- No rain falls inside, so paths don't need to act as drains.

**Limits**
- Don't exceed about 1.3 m; you reach about 60–65 cm from each side.
- Make beds along the low sides of a hoop roof narrower, or use them for short crops.
- Set the exact widths from the tunnel's inside width once it's built.

## Single-bed rain cover over a 1.2–1.3 m bed

Yes, it works. Size it by **crop height**, not bed width. The figures below are practical arithmetic, not sourced specifications.

| | Low crops (lettuce, greens, herbs, carrots) | Tall crops (tomato, cucumber, pepper) |
|---|---|---|
| Hoop or post span | About 1.4 m, just outside the bed edges or in the paths | About 1.4 m |
| Height at centre | 0.8–1.0 m | 1.6–2.0 m (posts and a ridge line) |
| Plastic width | About 1.8–2.0 m, top only, sides open | About 2.0–2.5 m, overhanging the bed edges against rain blown at an angle |
| Access | From the open sides, or lift a plastic section | Work underneath it |

**Considerations**
- **Runoff:** all the rain from the roof lands along both edges, in the paths. Keep the paths mulched and draining, and make beds 15 cm or higher.
- **Watering:** the covered soil gets no rain, so it needs drip or hand watering.
- **Wind:** a wide roof catches the strong dry-season winds. Use stakes or rebar driven well into the ground, tie the plastic tight, and run a ridge line along the top.
- **Several beds side by side:** one shelter over 2–3 beds costs less per m² than separate covers, and that brings you close to the tunnel.
