# Cilantro (Coriander) Transplant Fertilization — Worldwide Literature Review & Evidence-Based Recipe

> Compiled from peer-reviewed field/greenhouse trials and extension guidance worldwide.
> Focus: organic, living-soil approach for Volcán, Panama.
> Inputs available: urine-charged biochar (+3 g/L Epsom salt), citric-acid-treated bone ash, vermicast, Great White mycorrhizae, wood ash.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Cilantro (*Coriandrum sativum* L., Apiaceae) is a **light feeder** with a **taproot** that dislikes transplanting. Two findings correct common assumptions:

1. **Transplanting itself is the weak point.** Cilantro bolts in response to root disturbance nearly as readily as it does to heat. Direct seeding is preferred; if transplanting, move the whole rootball, don't bury the crown, and transplant at first-true-leaf stage.
2. **Cilantro is a strongly mycorrhiza-responsive host** — *not* "weakly mycorrhizal." Inoculation with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) produced growth equal to chemical fertilizer and raised shoot P by up to 254%.

The amendment strategy follows from cilantro's narrow tolerances: **pH 6.0–7.0**, **low EC**, and **low-moderate N**. The recipe therefore leans on vermicast + low-rate biochar, applies bone ash lightly (and never as raw acid slurry), values the mycorrhizae, and largely omits wood ash.

## 2. Cilantro's Agronomic Requirements (Literature)

| Parameter | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen** | Low demand: ~33 kg N per tonne of seed. Excess N → lodging, disease, delayed maturity, vegetative growth at the expense of oil/flavor. Optimum 20–90 kg N/ha. | Carrubba (2009); Rumińska (1983); Oliveira et al. (2003) |
| **N dose threshold** | 30 kg N/ha consistently best; yield declined at 90 kg/ha. | Turkish field trial (DergiPark, 2026) |
| **pH** | Growth/yield at par up to **pH 7.0**, declines beyond 7.5. Seed yield **5–6× lower** at alkaline pH. Above pH 9.0 the plant cannot complete its life cycle (chlorosis). Optimum **6.0–7.0**. | ICAR Int. J. Seed Spices (2021) |
| **Salinity (EC)** | Sensitive: each +1 dS/m reduces biomass ~3–6%. | SciELO Brazil hydroponic trials (2020, 2022) |
| **Mycorrhizae** | Strong host; AMF = chemical fertilizer in growth terms. See §4d. | IPP Portugal; Progress in Nutrition (2016) |
| **Transplanting** | Dislikes transplanting (taproot); transplant shock → bolting. | Univ. Hawai'i CTAHR; RHS (2026); OSU Extension; SARE |

**Design implication:** any combination of biochar + bone ash + wood ash pushes pH upward — the single biggest risk to this crop given the pH-7.0 ceiling.

## 3. Input-by-Input Literature Review

### 3.1 Urine-Charged Biochar (+ Epsom salt)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Urine:biochar 1:1 (v/v), applied 1.15–3 L/m² → **+60%** cabbage/kohlrabi yield | BUNCH, Bangladesh (Springer, 2021) |
| Urine-enhanced biochar, low-dose root-zone → **+300%** pumpkin yield | Schmidt et al., MDPI Agriculture 5:723 (2015) |
| **Urine-N adsorbed to biochar is NOT bio-available early** — slow release | Bischak et al., Elementa (2024) |
| Biochar-U residual N maintained yield in a second season | Frontiers in Soil Science (2026) |
| MgSO₄ boosts chlorophyll, gas exchange, and leaf yield; Mg sulfate most effective form | Borowski & Michałek, Acta Agrobotanica (2010) |
| **Excess Mg suppresses K and Ca uptake** | Guan et al., MDPI Agronomy 10:1863 (2020) |
| Container biochar: 25–50% v/v generally safe; 75–100% can reduce growth (high pH/EC, N-binding) | MDPI Horticulturae 5:14 (2019) review |

**Reading:** urine-biochar is a *slow-release* N source — it is not a bolting trigger. The 3 g/L Epsom is a modest, appropriate Mg dose; do not add more (over-Mg antagonizes K/Ca). Keep biochar ≤10% v/v in the hole.

### 3.2 Bone Ash Processed with Citric Acid

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Citric acid effectively solubilizes apatite-P: 15% CA → water-extractable P 18.7 g/kg | Raniro et al., J. Mater. Cycles Waste Manag. (2026) |
| Bone ash citric-soluble P ≈ 82,040 mg/kg — highest among bone ash/bone char/phosphate rock | Saleh et al. (2023) |
| Bone ash outperformed bone char and phosphate rock for available-P release in soil | Sci. Rep. 15 (2025) |
| **Caution: acidified bone ash applied raw to soil did NOT increase P uptake or biomass, and could cause biomass losses** | Raniro et al. (under review, cited 2026) |
| Biochars need ~20–30% more acid to reach the same pH (higher Ca²⁺ buffering) | Raniro et al. (2026) |

**Reading:** citric-acid treatment makes the P available, but **fresh, low-pH acid slurry is not a transplant amendment.** It must be buffered (pH 6+) — ideally aged into vermicast/compost — before it touches roots or AMF. Applied lightly and buried below the rootball, the aged material is a sound P + Ca source for taproot establishment.

### 3.3 Vermicast

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 2.5 t/ha VC + 40 kg N + 20 kg P₂O₅ → max seed/stover/oil yield | Arid Rajasthan field trial (chemijournal, 2017) |
| 5 m³/fed VC → best branches, herb weight, fruit yield, oil | Al-Azhar Univ., Egypt (2025) |
| 9 t/ha VC → max biomass | Firouzkuh, Iran (ijabbr) |
| VC + Azotobacter + AMF → superior growth, yield, volatile oil | Egypt (2025), doi:10.64064/1319-1039.1021 |
| Typical VC in coriander trial: EC 3.2 dS/m, pH 6.8, N 3.1%, P₂O₅ 1.2%, K₂O 0.89% | MDPI Agronomy 11:1427 (2021) |

**Reading:** vermicast is the reliable workhorse for cilantro. Its only risk is **EC** — at high rates it can push salinity on a salt-sensitive crop. 15–20% of hole volume is the practical ceiling.

### 3.4 Great White (Arbuscular Mycorrhizae + bacteria + Trichoderma)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| *Rhizophagus irregularis*: 25–38% root colonization; **+254% shoot P, +44% N, +27% K, +82% chlorophyll**; growth = chemical fertilizer | IPP Portugal (recipp.ipp.pt) |
| *Glomus mosseae*: increased yield, essential oil, and linalool | Progress in Nutrition (2016) |
| Fungal consortium (PI + RI + DV) → essential oil yield **+107%** | Univ. Tabriz (2023) |
| **Soluble P reduces colonization 38% → 25%** (chemical fertilizer suppressed AMF) | IPP Portugal; Progress in Nutrition (2016) |
| Baseline colonization range for cilantro: 11–43% | Schroeder & Janos (2004) |

**Reading:** cilantro responds strongly to AMF — the Great White endo component (*Glomus intraradices/Rhizophagus, G. mosseae, G. aggregatum, G. etunicatum*) is genuinely useful, and the bacteria/Trichoderma add damping-off suppression. But **soluble P suppresses colonization** — so keep the bone ash light and keep it away from the root-dusting zone.

### 3.5 Wood Ash

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| pH 9–13.5; CaCO₃ equivalent 25–60%; average NPK ≈ 0-1-3 | UNH Extension (2018); UGA Extension B1142 |
| Raises soil pH *faster* than limestone | UNH Extension (2018) |
| "Too much wood ash can raise pH higher than optimal, negatively impacting nutrient uptake" | UNH Extension (2018) |
| On acid soil: K in solution rose 17→650 mg/L, biomass 3.3× — but pH rose 4.5→7.2 | MDPI Agronomy 15:1097 (2025) |
| Optimal doses 7.5–30 g/kg soil; excess causes P/micronutrient lock-up | MDPI IJERPH 16:1246 (2019) |

**Reading:** wood ash is a real K/Ca liming agent, best on *acid* soils. Given cilantro's pH ceiling of 7.0 — and the already-alkaline biochar + bone ash in this recipe — wood ash should be **omitted unless a soil test shows pH < 6.0**, and then only at trace rates.

## 4. Evidence-Based Transplant Recipe

Per plant (≈1 L amended planting hole / 4–6" pot equivalent):

| Input | Amount | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vermicast** | ~150–200 ml (⅔–¾ cup) | Base fertility + biology + hormones | ~15–20% of hole; EC is the ceiling |
| **Urine-charged biochar** | ~50 ml (3 tbsp) | Slow-release N + Mg + habitat | ~5%; slow-release, not a bolting risk |
| **Bone ash (citric-acid, *aged/buffered*)** | **1 tsp (5 g)** | P + Ca for taproot | Bottom of hole; never fresh acid slurry; light dose to protect AMF |
| **Great White** | ¼ tsp dusted on roots | Endo-AMF + bacteria + Trichoderma | Apply to roots, not into bone-ash layer |
| **Wood ash** | **0** (¼ tsp only if pH < 6.0) | K + pH | Omit; pH ceiling is 7.0 |

### Assembly (bottom → top)

1. **Bottom of hole:** mix 1 tsp aged bone ash into a small scoop of soil/vermicast.
2. **Fill:** blend vermicast + biochar + native soil (≈20% / 5% / 75%).
3. **Plant:** move the *entire rootball* intact; keep the crown at soil line — do not bury deep.
4. **Inoculate:** dust Great White on the exposed roots at planting.
5. **Water in** with plain dechlorinated water (or dilute LAB/compost tea). **No liquid N until established.**

### Key interactions (from the literature)

1. **Soluble P suppresses AMF** — keep bone ash light and separated from the inoculant.
2. **Alkaline inputs add up** — biochar + bone ash + wood ash all raise pH; cilantro's ceiling is 7.0. The citric-acid bone ash partially offsets this.
3. **Don't over-N** — excess N weakens flavor and hastens bolting; the urine-biochar is slow-release, so the 5% rate is safe.
4. **Transplant shock ≈ bolting** — minimize root disturbance; direct-seed future successions.
