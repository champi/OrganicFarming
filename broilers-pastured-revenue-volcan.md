# Organic / pastured broilers — high revenue on little land (lit review 2026-11-23)

Status: research only, no decision. Own synthesis flagged **[own]**.

## Bottom line
- Revenue is set by **price + marketing + labour efficiency**, not land. Land is rarely the binding constraint below ~1,000 birds/yr.
- Commodity price → loss. Premium direct price → profit (NC State/Poultry Sci 2026: −$5.56/bird at $1.32/lb; +$15.92/bird at $6.09/lb).
- NY 36-farm study (SARE LNE22-435, 2022-24): direct cost $20/bird, all-in $35; +$1.26/bird before labour, −$13 with labour @ $15/h. 69 % profitable without labour, 26 % with. Higher-priced farms = more profitable.
- Experienced: ~10 min labour/bird; beginner >1 h/bird (CIAS Wisconsin).

## Low-space option: house + small yard, organic feed, no routine medicine (owner request 2026-11-23)
### Legal minimum space per bird
| Rule | House (indoor) | Outdoor | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Panama organic, fixed house** | ≤ 10 birds/m², ≤ 20 kg/m² (text says "mínimo", EU original = max 21 kg) | **4 m²/bird** | DE 121/2015 Anexo 6 |
| **Panama organic, mobile house** | ≤ 16 birds/m², ≤ 30 kg/m² | **2.5 m²/bird** | DE 121/2015 Anexo 6 |
| EU organic (current) | same as Panama; mobile ≤ 150 m² floor; ≤ 4,800 birds/compartment; pop-holes 4 m/100 m² | 4 m² fixed | Reg. 2020/464 Art. 15 + Annex I Part IV |
| USDA organic (from 2025/2029) | ≤ 5 lb/ft² or ≥ 2 ft² (0.19 m²)/bird | ≥ 2 ft² (0.19 m²)/bird, ≥ 75 % soil + vegetation; tractors count as outdoor | 7 CFR 205.241(b)(10), (c)(6) |
| Panama nitrogen cap | 580 broilers/ha (170 kg N/ha/yr) | — | DE 121/2015 Anexo 6, Art. 63; = EU 889/2008 Annex IV |
- Panama Art. 77: poultry free-moving, outdoor access when climate allows, no cages. Art. 23: no confinement-only organic. Art. 81: empty house + rest outdoor area between batches → ≥ 2 yards used alternately **[own]**.
- At 2.5-3 kg live, the kg cap binds: fixed house ≈ 7-8 birds/m² (0.13-0.15 m²/bird); mobile ≈ 10-12 birds/m² (0.09 m²/bird).
- 580/ha cap: EU guidance computes it from N excreted (INAO 2022); **[own]** read as average places present → ~230 places on 0.4 ha; manure exported to other organic land under a written agreement can extend it in EU (889/2008 Art. 3(3)); Panama equivalent not found.

### "No medicine" — what the rules and evidence say
- Panama Art. 46: vaccines allowed; no systematic preventive medication; Art. 45: sick birds must be treated (bird loses organic status). → "no routine medicine", not "never treat".
- Organic: anticoccidial drugs banned; only live coccidiosis vaccine (Cervantes & McDougald 2023). Small low-density flocks often manage without (NCAT).
- Coccidiosis risk rises with density and wet litter; target litter ~25 % moisture, 10-15 cm deep; feeders never empty; brood separately ≤ 3 wk; ≥ 1 ft² (0.09 m²)/chick in brooder (NCAT, Merck Coccivac guide).
- Volcán RH 90-94 % May-Nov → wet litter = main risk in a small house **[own]**: roof overhang, raised dry floor, open-sided ventilation, deep litter.
- Mortality tracks humidity/temperature/litter more than density within 30-46 kg/m² (Dawkins 2004, 2.7 M birds); organic densities (20-30 kg/m²) are below that range.

## Volcán-specific risks
### Climate vs broiler needs (2nd pass 2026-11-23; supersedes 1st-pass "15-25 %" figure)
| | Volcán | David (lowland) | Broiler target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean | 16-17 °C | ~25 °C | — |
| Night low | 13-15 °C | 20-22 °C | brood 30-35 °C wk 1 → 20 °C after d 27 (Ross 2025) |
| Day high | 20-22 °C (climate-data); 21-24 °C (WeatherSpark); MIDA 16-27 °C range | 29-33 °C | thermoneutral 18-22/24 °C; mild heat stress ≥25 °C |
| RH | 74-94 % (May-Nov ≥ 90 %) | 63-84 % | — |
| Rain | 4,140-4,405 mm/yr, wet May-Nov | — | — |
- Heat: lowlands exceed thermoneutral every day → heat stress. Volcán days ≈ ideal finishing temperature. Owner correct.
- Volcán problem is the other side: cold, wet nights (13-15 °C, rain) + altitude.
- Altitude threshold: Ross ≥ 1,000 m = "high altitude"; Hubbard: worsens progressively > 1,200 m; Arce/Marín: mainly > 1,300 m.
- Severity scales with altitude. 15-50 % mortality figures come from 2,000-2,800 m (Bogotá, Mexico, Andes). Near 1,400 m: Tabriz farms (~1,350-1,400 m) mean ascites mortality 4.4 % (1.4-10.7 %), main cause poor ventilation. 1,720 m: 8.6 % ♀ / 13.8 % ♂ at normal temp, 44 % ♂ when cold (Özkan 2010).
- Cool + damp = main ascites trigger on pasture (APPPA). Cool/wet Puyallup WA: Cornish Cross 12 % vs slow Cornish 6.7 % mortality. NY 36 farms: no CC vs slow-growth mortality difference; predators = top loss.
- **[own] estimate for Volcán, Cornish Cross on pasture: ascites a few % extra with good management, ≥ 10 % with cold/wet nights or poor brooding.** No study at ~1,400 m in humid tropics on pasture.
- Mitigation (sourced): brood 34-35 °C wk 1 at altitude (Hubbard); windproof, dry shelter at night (curtains/tarps); intermittent lighting 1L:3D d3-14 or d10-21 → −33/−50 % ascites, increasing photoperiod → −61 %, no final-weight loss (Hassanzadeh 2003, 2005); ventilation without drafts; mash rather than pellets (APPPA); slaughter 6-8 wk (UMaine); females lower risk.
- Strain choice = economics, not climate veto. Cornish Cross: 7.4 wk, FCR 2.10 (NY). Slow growth: 10.7 wk, FCR 2.47, ~+50 % time; fewer heart/leg losses, better forager. Medium (Freedom Ranger/Redbro 9-10 wk) between. Coloured strains available in Panama before (Sasso, Biogranjas Coclé 2014, chicks $0.80). **[own]** Trial: 1 batch each, record mortality cause by necropsy.
- **"Orgánico" is a regulated claim** (Ley 8/2002, DE 121/2015, Sello Orgánico Panamá). Livestock/poultry certification only being rolled out by MIDA since 2025 (34 certified operators, all crops). Certified organic feed not found locally. → Market as "pastoreo"/"pasture-raised" unless certified; verify with MIDA Unidad Agropecuaria Orgánica.
- **Slaughter:** Panama technical norm defines processed chicken as slaughtered in authorised plants. Small-producer exemption not found → ask MIDA / Agencia Panameña de Alimentos (Ley 206/2021) before selling dressed birds. Lack of legal slaughter = main reason 8 of 11 Brazilian caipira ventures closed (Embrapa 2015).
