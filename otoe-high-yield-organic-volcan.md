# Otoe (*Xanthosoma*): High-Yield Organic Growing for Volcán — Worldwide Literature Review

> **Literature review, Nov 2026.** What drives otoe (tannia, cocoyam, yautía, quequisque, macabo, tiquisque) yield in conventional and organic systems worldwide, and how to get high yields organically in Volcán.
> Companion files: [otoe-nutrition-per-plant-volcan.md](otoe-nutrition-per-plant-volcan.md) (per-plant input doses), [taro-otoe-cooking-growing-volcan.md](taro-otoe-cooking-growing-volcan.md) (taxonomy, climate fit, sourcing).

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Organic can match or beat conventional for otoe.** In Kerala, organic nutrition (manure + wood ash) out-yielded integrated organic + chemical management for two years running, and organic aroids/yams averaged +10–20% yield over chemical farming at ICAR-CTCRI.
2. **Root rot (*Pythium myriotylum*) is the yield killer, not nutrients.** It can cause losses of up to 90%. Volcanic Andosols are naturally **suppressive** thanks to organic matter and microbes, but they lose it after ~5 years of continuous otoe. Rotation, clean planting material and mature compost/vermicompost are the core defences. The purple (red) type, which includes the San Andrés clone, is more tolerant than the white type.
3. **Water and drainage are the next lever.** A 10% water deficit cuts yield by 24–33%, while a high water table (15 cm) gave 40% less yield than 45 cm. Otoe needs to be constantly moist but never waterlogged.
4. **Planting material and density.** Use 150–200 g corm setts (or top-half corms) from vigorous, healthy plants. Denser planting (up to 2.8 plants/m²) raised marketable yield per area by +50% in Ghana, though each plant yields less.
5. **Volcán-specific limits:** cold (otoe wants >20 °C mean, Volcán is ~18 °C), weeds in the first 5.5 months, shade (50% shade = −67% yield) and early leaf picking (before ~20–23 weeks, leaf harvest cuts cormels by up to 59%).
6. **Yield targets:** world average ~8.6 t/ha, Panama ~6 t/ha. Research plots reach 20–34 t/ha, and leaving Morada in the ground for 24 instead of 12 months raised tuber yield from 11.5 to 28 t/ha.

| World average (FAO 2007) | 8.6 t/ha | Manner (agroforestry.net) |

**Per plant:** at ~1–1.4 plants/m², 20 t/ha ≈ 1.5–2 kg per plant. A home-garden goal for Volcán is ~1 kg of cormels per plant in 12 months, more if left longer.

## 3. Organic vs conventional

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Organic (FYM 37.5 t/ha + wood ash 2 t/ha, 2/3 basal + 3 splits at 2, 4, 6 months) beat INM (FYM 25 t/ha + 80:50:150 NPK) on cormel number, cormel yield per plant and soil properties, both years | Jayapal & Swadija 2020, 2021 |
| "Tannia is more adapted to organic cultivation." FYM 12.5 t/ha + ash 3 t/ha was best for growth and yield | Suja, Susan John & Sundaresan 2009 |
| Organic aroids and yams: +10–20% yield, +20–40% net profit, higher dry matter, K, Ca, Mg; lower oxalate | Suja et al. 2012, 2015 (IntechOpen) |
| CTCRI organic package: FYM 20 t/ha (1.6 kg/pit) + neem cake 1 t/ha (80–85 g/pit) at planting; cowpea green manure sown between pits (20 kg seed/ha), incorporated at 45–60 days (15–20 t/ha green matter); ash 2 t/ha (160–165 g/plant) at incorporation | CTCRI Tech Bulletin 99 (2025) |
| Sawdust mulch 20 t/ha alone: +36–52%; mulch + NPK always beat NPK alone | Shiyam 2007 |
| Poultry manure 25–30 t/ha ≥ NPK 125–150 kg/ha on growth and yield | Gwan 2025 |
| Biochar 10–30 t/ha raised yield on degraded sand; biochar + poultry manure was best | Adekiya 2020 |

**Agreement:** otoe responds strongly to organic matter. Every organic package uses lots of manure (12.5–37.5 t/ha ≈ 1–3 kg per plant), **wood ash** as the K source, and splits at 2, 4 and 6 months with earthing-up.
**Disagreement:** CTCRI and Kerala call otoe an **"indicator plant for Mg deficiency"** and use dolomite at 1 t/ha. But that is on acid laterite. On Volcán's Mg-rich Andisol, the Puerto Rico Mg trial (no response) is more relevant.

## 4. Root rot (*Pythium myriotylum*, "mal seco") — the main yield limit

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Losses up to 90%. Early infection stunts; late infection (5–6 months) kills feeder roots | Adiobo 2007; Oni et al. review |
| **Andosols around Mount Cameroon are suppressive.** Higher OM, Ca, K, Mg, N and microbes; suppression partly lost on sterilizing, regained on re-colonization | Adiobo et al. 2007 (*Soil Biol. Biochem.*) |
| Andosols become **conducive after ~5 years of continuous cocoyam** | Adiobo 2007 |
| Mature, manure-based **vermicompost** gave the lowest incidence; mature manure compost + vermicompost the lowest severity | Artavia et al. 2010 (Costa Rica) |
| ***Tithonia diversifolia*** **compost + poultry manure** was the most suppressive of 8 composts (24.5% incidence), then *Chromolaena* | Djeugap et al. 2014, 2016 (Cameroon) |
| *Trichoderma asperellum* (PR10, PR11) cut infection by 50% | Mbarga et al. 2012 |
| Fluorescent *Pseudomonas* (CMR12a, CMR5c) control the disease. The **red (purple) cocoyam has field tolerance** and a more diverse protective rhizosphere | Perneel 2007; Oni et al. 2019 |
| Dry root rot occurs mostly in monocultures with water or nutrient stress; good water management reduces it | Goenaga 1993; PR studies |
| Choose fields with no otoe for the last 2–3 years | CTCRI 2025 |

**For Volcán:** the soil type is already the suppressive kind. Keep it that way with high organic matter, rotation (≥2–3 years between otoe on a spot) and mature vermicast/compost at planting. The *Tithonia* already on the buy list ([seeds-to-buy.md](seeds-to-buy.md)) makes the best-tested suppressive compost. Great White contains *Trichoderma* and *Bacillus*, but no trial has tested it on *Pythium* in otoe.

## 5. Water and drainage

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| 10% ET deficit → −24 to −33% yield | PR 20-year irrigation analysis (2014) |
| Drip at high water regime 10.6 t/ha vs 6.6 and 5.3 t/ha at lower regimes; the difference was in cormel **number** | PR 1987 |
| Replacing 132% of ET gave the top cormel yield (19.5 t/ha) in a semiarid site | Goenaga (PR) |
| Water table 15 cm → 9.9 t/ha; 45 cm → 16.6 t/ha | PR Mollisol study |
| Needs ~1,800–2,000 mm evenly distributed; doesn't tolerate waterlogging, unlike taro | CTCRI 2025; MIDA |

**For Volcán:** ~4,400 mm/yr is plenty in Apr–Dec, so drain with ridges or a sloped bed, never a dip. **In the Jan–Apr dry season, irrigate** if the crop is still growing. That's when a deficit costs the most.

## 6. Soil preparation and planting geometry

- **Deep tillage to 30 cm + pit planting** gave the highest cormel number and yield. Pits beat mounds in Kerala (Jayapal 2020). CARDI instead advises **ridges** (45 cm high, 90 cm base) on the contour, and not mounds, for drainage. Both reject plain mounds. On a wet slope, the ridge or a raised bed is the safer choice.
- **Earthing-up raises marketable cormels.** Two earthings-up gave +70% commercial cormels in mangarito (*X. mafaffa*) (Heredia Zárate, Brazil). Kerala earths up at 2, 4 and 6 months.
- **Density:** 2.78 plants/m² (≈60 × 60 cm) gave +50.6% marketable cormels, and 1.78/m² +19.3%, vs 1.23/m² (Hotsonyame & Ahiekpor, Ghana). Per-plant cormel weight fell at high density but cormel size didn't. Used elsewhere: PR 1 × 0.7 m, Kerala 0.75 × 0.75 m, CTCRI 0.9 × 0.9 m, MIDA 1.0 × 0.45 m.
  - **For Volcán:** ~0.75 × 0.75 m (1.8/m²) balances yield against airflow in the wet.
- **Rice husk or coir pith 500 g/plant** as a soil conditioner raised yield, lowered bulk density and raised porosity (Jayapal 2020). Yield correlated negatively with bulk density.

## 7. Planting material

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Corm setts **150–200 g** (3–4 buds, 6–8 × 5–6 cm) or cormels 50–80 g; 1.8–2.5 t/ha of corms needed | ISRC seed standards; CTCRI 2025 |
| Corm setts give quicker, higher yields; plant tops are slowest and lowest | Manner; Weightman & Moros 1982 |
| Whole corm and top-half corm sprouted best and gave the most corm yield; cormels had lower sprouting and more non-marketable cormels | De Chavez et al. 2019 (Philippines, volcanic soil) |
| Setts with a 15–30 cm petiole established well; keeping the leafy shoot on the sett reduced survival | De Chavez 2019 |
| **Minisetts (~15 g)** from mother corms raised in a nursery for a month, then transplanted. This frees all cormels for sale | CTCRI 2025 |
| Setts from the older, less succulent part of the corm establish better (less rot) | Enyi 1967 (in De Chavez) |
| Tissue-culture (virus-free) plants were more vigorous; claimed higher yield | Cameroon 1997; Cuba; CTCRI |
| Sprout corms in moist sawdust over plastic, then cut into 100–150 g bud pieces | St Lucia Tech Pack 2017 |

**For Volcán:** select the most vigorous, healthy San Andrés plants at harvest. Use their mother corms as 150–200 g setts (or 15 g minisetts in a nursery to multiply stock), and sell or eat the cormels. Pre-sprout in moist sawdust so only live setts go into the cold soil.

## 8. Light, shade and temperature

- **Full sun for yield.** 50% shade from trees or bananas cut yield by 67% (Morton 1972, in Manner). Otoe tolerates light shade (it yields under young oil palm and in agroforests), but shade is a yield cost, not a benefit.
- **Temperature:** optimum mean >20 °C (CGIAR), EcoCrop 20–28 °C (absolute 10–35 °C). At 1,200 m in Java, plants grew less than at 98 m (Indonesian altitude study 2016). Volcán (~1,378 m, ~18 °C) is at the cool edge, so expect slower growth.
- **Warming the soil:** black plastic mulch raised soil temperature by ~2 °C and taro yield by 72% (Ezeaku 2007). Thick organic mulch cools the soil. In Volcán, use a thin organic mulch or black plastic on the ridge. My inference: no trial at this altitude.
- **Plant at the start of the warm, wet season** (late Apr–May in Volcán).

---

## 9. Weeds

- Weed competition in the **first 5.5 months** hurt yield the most (Puerto Rico tanier trial, ISTRC). After the canopy closes, otoe shades out weeds (Manner).
- **For Volcán:** hand-weed or mulch hard through month 5–6, then ease off.

---

## 10. Leaf harvesting

- Picking all leaves cut cormel yield by 59%, picking alternate leaves by 31% (KNUST, Ghana).
- Removing any leaf **before ~23 weeks** lowered cormels. After that, up to 4 leaves could go without loss. Starting leaf harvest at 20 weeks gave 39% more cormels than starting at 12 weeks.
- Intensive leaf harvest gave the highest **combined** value (leaves + cormels).
- **For Volcán:** take leaves only after ~5 months, at most 1–2 per plant at a time, unless you're selling leaves.

---

## 11. Harvest, ratoon and storage

- **Harvest** when the older leaves yellow, at 9–12 months (purple types mature later than white). Corms keep well in the ground, so harvest anywhere from 6 to 12+ months. Leaving them through a second wet season risks sprouting and watery corms (CTCRI 2025).
- **A longer crop yields more.** Morada at 24 months gave 2.5× the tubers of 12 months (Vicente-Chandler 1982).
- **Sequential ("milking") harvest:** from 6 months, dig cormels beside the plant without uprooting it. Common in small plantations (St Lucia Tech Pack).
- **Curing and storage:** cure 4–5 days in the sun; store embedded in dry sand or soil in a dark, ventilated place for 4–5 months, or at 7 °C and 80–85% RH. **Below 5 °C causes chilling injury** (CTCRI 2025).

---

## 12. Synthesis: high-yield organic recipe for Volcán

1. **Site:** the warmest, full-sun spot with no otoe for ≥2–3 years; contour ridges or a raised bed; water table >45 cm.
2. **Soil:** dig 30 cm deep. Per plant: ~1.5–2 kg manure/compost equivalent (vermicast + compost; *Tithonia*-poultry compost when available), 500 g rice husk, plus the bone ash and biochar from [otoe-nutrition-per-plant-volcan.md](otoe-nutrition-per-plant-volcan.md).
3. **Material:** 150–200 g pre-sprouted San Andrés corm setts from the healthiest plants; Great White on the sett.
4. **Spacing:** ~0.75 × 0.75 m; plant late Apr–May.
5. **Months 0–6:** weed-free; earth up at months 2, 4 and 6 with each organic top-dress (SOP/urine schedule in the nutrition guide); cowpea green manure between plants, cut in at 45–60 days.
6. **Water:** irrigate through any dry spell. A 10% deficit costs a quarter of the yield.
7. **Mulch:** thin, or black plastic on the ridge for soil warmth.
8. **Leaves:** none before 5 months.
9. **Harvest:** 10–12 months, or milk cormels from month 6–8 and leave the plant to keep producing.

---

## 13. Gaps

- No yield trial for otoe at ~1,400 m or ~18 °C. All targets for Volcán are extrapolated.
- The suppressive-Andosol results come from Cameroon. Whether Barú Andisols behave the same is untested.
- Field (not pot) evidence for compost and biocontrol against root rot is thin.
- Clean planting material: the yield gain is claimed but poorly quantified in the field.
- No published MIDA/IDIAP yield or fertilizer data for San Andrés was found online.
