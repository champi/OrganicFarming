# Farm Rules to Remember

Lessons we always want to apply, learned on the farm or from research. Check this list before buying seeds and before planning a planting. Details live in the linked files.

## Seeds and varieties

### Always pair a female-heavy variety with a male-heavy pollen partner

**Rule:** when we buy a squash variety that makes mostly female flowers (winter-greenhouse hybrids, "short day / cold set" types, parthenocarpic types), also buy and sow a variety **of the same species** that reliably makes male flowers. The same goes for any variety the seller says "needs a pollinator".

**Why:** in Nov 2026 our 4 × President F1 zucchini (Seminis, sold by L'Ortolano) made almost only female flowers on 3 plants. The variety is bred for winter greenhouses: it sets fruit in short days and cold, and is partly parthenocarpic. Volcán's cool, cloudy, short-day season pushes the same way. Our pollen came only from plant #4, and only because a cat had damaged its growing tip. Without that plant, many females would have had no pollen. Almería and northern-European growers deal with this the same way: they plant a male-heavy "pollenizer" variety beside the main one. See [zucchini-trial-2026-president-f1.md](zucchini-trial-2026-president-f1.md) (log from Nov 13 on).

**How to apply:**
- Pollen must come from the **same species**. Zucchini (*Cucurbita pepo*) is pollinated only by *C. pepo* (other zucchini, pattypan, acorn, most pumpkins sold as *pepo*), not by *C. moschata* or *C. maxima*.
- Ratio: about **1 male-heavy plant per 4–5 female-heavy plants**. One male flower can pollinate 5 or more females, but each then gets less pollen. Less pollen means fewer seeds, and the fruit stays small with a pointed tip. When males are scarce, hand-pollinate with **1 fresh male per 1–2 females**, before 9–10 am, until every stigma lobe is visibly yellow ([zucchini-pollination-fruit-shape.md](zucchini-pollination-fruit-shape.md)).
- Sow the pollen partner **about 1 week before** the main variety, so its male flowers are open when the first females open.
- Prefer an open-pollinated or older variety for the pollen plant. Older, open-pollinated summer squash usually start with male flowers, and many modern hybrids start with females.
- Before buying, read the seller or breeder sheet for words such as "winter greenhouse", "short day", "cold set", "parthenocarpic" or "fruit set without bees". Any of these means the variety needs a pollen partner.
- Already used for pumpkins: 1 Winter Sweet per 4 Tetsukabuto, sown 7 days earlier ([seeds-to-buy.md](seeds-to-buy.md)).

**Backup when there are no male flowers:** pick a male bud the day before it opens, pull off the petals, and keep it in a closed box in the fridge on a damp paper towel, without the pollen touching the towel. Let it warm for 1 h before use. Refrigerated flowers still set fruit after 48 h (Univ. Almería), and buds stored this way worked for up to about 2 weeks in one study (Wang & Robinson, CGC 1983). Pollinate within 3 h of opening.
