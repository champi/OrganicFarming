# Zucchini (Cucurbita pepo) in Volcán — Establishment Method, Spacing & Low Tunnels

> **Volcán, Chiriquí, Panama. ~1,400m elevation. 14–24°C (night lows ~14°C), high rainfall (rainy season May–Jan, dry season Jan–May), 74–94% humidity.**

---

## TL;DR

1. **"Never transplant zucchini — direct sow is better" is only half-true.** The real mistake is transplanting *old, rootbound* seedlings (>30 days). Young transplants (~21 days) reliably give **earlier harvest** and often **equal-or-higher yield** than direct sowing.
2. **In Volcán the constraint is *cool temperature* (slow growth), not a frost deadline.** 14°C nights are *below* zucchini's 18–24°C optimum, so the plant grows slowly year-round — and a transplant's head start still pays off.
3. **Spacing:** commercial "max tons/ha" = dense (0.3–0.45 m). For an organic family garden in humid Volcán, **go wide: ~0.9 m between plants, 1.0–1.2 m between rows** (disease/airflow wins over tonnage).
4. **Low tunnel:** classic zucchini tunnel is **18" (45 cm) high × 30" (75 cm) wide**. In Volcán, use it as a **vented rain/pest shelter, not a sealed heat trap** — sealed plastic in 85–95% RH = mildew + slugs.

---

## 1. Transplant vs. Direct Sow — What the Literature Actually Says

### The mechanism behind the "don't transplant" folklore (partly true)

Zucchini (*Cucurbita pepo*) is genuinely sensitive to **root disturbance**. It develops a long **taproot** within days and becomes **rootbound faster than tomato/pepper** — it can't be "potted up" like a tomato. Start it too early indoors and you get a 30+ day-old, rootbound, stressed seedling whose taproot is damaged at planting → it "sulks" for weeks. This is the real origin of the folklore.

Root restriction is well documented in the species: NeSmith's root-restriction work (1993, *J. Plant Nutr.*) showed smaller containers restrict leaf area, dry-weight accumulation, and fruit yield in 'Dixie' and 'Senator' summer squash.

### But transplants win when timed right

**NeSmith 1993** (*HortScience* 28:618) — the key zucchini-species trial:
- Compared 10 / 21 / 30-day-old transplants vs. direct-seeded control.
- 28–35 day-old transplants grew *slower* after transplanting than 10/14/21-day-olds (rootbound).
- **10-day-olds were too underdeveloped** — hard to remove from cells, prone to breakage.
- **21 days = the "sweet spot"** — near-optimum growth, ~10 days of planting flexibility.
- **All transplants flowered and yielded 10–15 days earlier than direct seeding**, and produced more fruit.

**Brown et al. 1996** — zucchini-species field trial: **transplants produced +51% fruit weight** over direct-seeded squash.

**Castagnino / INTA (Villa Mercedes, Argentina)** — mixed zucchini (*C. pepo*) + *C. maxima* trial: transplanting **+55% fruit, +77% kg/ha**, and advanced harvest ~7 days.

### Important caveat: "transplants always win" is NOT universal

The strongest "transplants don't always help" results come from **other cucurbit species**, not zucchini:

| Study | Species | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **De Grazia et al. 2005** | *C. maxima* (zapallito) | Total yield *not significantly* affected — only earliness improved |
| **Wissocq 2002** | *C. maxima* (likely) | Transplant *reduced* earliness & fruit count |
| **Egel et al. 2008** (*HortScience* 43:1410) | Watermelon (*Citrullus*) | Direct-seeded **outyielded** transplants |

So the *earliness* advantage is consistent across all cucurbits, but the *total-yield* advantage is species-dependent — for zucchini itself, the yield evidence leans *toward* transplants.

### The root-architecture mechanism (biological, NOT weather)

Best documented in watermelon (Elmstrom 1973; Egel 2008), and assumed to generalize across cucurbits:

- **Direct-sown** → strong, dominant **taproot** (geotropic, deep). Deep water access + drought resilience, but fewer fine roots in the topsoil.
- **Transplanted** → taproot curls in the cell and "typically does not recover geotropic growth, tends to grow horizontally" → a **shallow, fibrous root system** that is **superior at scavenging water + nutrients from the topsoil** where compost/fertilizer sits.

Trade-off for you: **dry season (Jan–May) → deep taproot (direct sow) is an asset; wet season with heavy compost top-dressing → fibrous roots (transplant) shine.**

## 2. Why Weather Matters in Volcán (even with no frost)

Zucchini's **optimal growth range is 18–24°C** (UC Davis; Indonesian highland phenology study). Below ~15–16°C growth nearly stops and germination becomes poor (KZN Dept. Agriculture).

**Volcán's "never below 14°C" is a COOL climate for zucchini, not a warm one.** The plant spends much of its life at or below optimum, growing **slowly year-round**. So the transplant advantage here is real — not because the *season is short*, but because **growth is slow (cool)**, and a ~3-week-old transplant has already banked thermal time (growing degree-days) that a direct-sown seed must still earn slowly in cool soil.

The real **seasonality in Volcán is wet/dry, not temperature**: rainy season = disease/pest pressure on vulnerable seedlings; dry season = drought stress that favors a deep taproot.

**Bottom line for Volcán (organic family garden):**
- **Transplant young (~3-week) seedlings in biodegradable pots** (Jiffy-7 / newspaper / peat — plant the whole plug, bury the rim) if you want **earlier fruit, better wet-season survival, and the fibrous-root feeding advantage**.
- **Direct sow** if you want **low labor, year-round succession planting, and drought-resilient deep roots through the dry season**.
- **Never transplant old (>30-day, rootbound) seedlings** — that's the actual mistake.

## 3. Spacing — Dense vs. Wide Depends on the Goal

### The density studies (zucchini-specific, *C. pepo*)

**Dweikat & Kostewicz 1989** (*HortScience* 24:86) — the definitive spacing trial:
- Tested 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60 / 0.75 m within-row, single vs. double rows.
- **Total yield per hectare increased as spacing *decreased*** (0.75 → 0.30 m).
- **But early yield *decreased*** with closer spacing (competition delays early fruit).
- Optimum: **~37,000 plants/ha** = double rows 0.30 m apart, plants 0.45 m apart staggered, + 202 kg N/ha.

**Powell, Stoffella & Paris 1993** (*HortScience* 28:796):
- Spacing 30.5 → 76.2 cm: **fruit/ha ↑ with closer spacing, fruit/plant ↑ with wider spacing.**
- No effect on whitefly/silverleaf/ZYMV incidence. Populations >30,000 plants/ha gave highest yields.

**Brazilian Italian-zucchini trial (SciELO):** cultivar-dependent — hybrid *Alícia* max 28,490 kg/ha at **0.64 m**; *Caserta* max at **0.40 m**.

**Extension standard (OSU Oregon):** rows **36–40" (0.9–1.0 m)**, plants **18–36" (0.45–0.9 m)** in-row.

### Recommendation

| Goal | Spacing |
|---|---|
| **Max total tons/ha (commercial)** | Dense: 0.30–0.45 m in-row, double rows, ~37,000 plants/ha. Smaller fruit, less per plant, later early yield. |
| **Broad recommendation** | 0.45–0.9 m (18–36") in-row, 0.9–1.2 m (36–48") between rows. |
| **Home / organic family garden, humid Volcán** | **Wider: ~0.9 m (3 ft) between plants, 1.0–1.2 m (3.5–4 ft) between rows.** |

**Why wider in Volcán:** dense zucchini in 85–95% humidity is a **powdery-mildew invitation** — tight canopies trap moisture and stall airflow. Wider spacing → better airflow, bigger/easier-to-pick fruit, healthier individual plants. The "maximize plants/ha" density data is about squeezing tons from a field, not producing quality organic food for a family in a wet highland.

## 4. Low Tunnel — Height & Volcán-Specific Cautions

### Height numbers

- **Classic zucchini tunnel (UC Small Farms):** **18" (45 cm) high × ~30" (75 cm) wide** at the base, on wire hoops. Zucchini squash is explicitly listed as a crop successfully grown under these.
- **Early-establishment tunnels (UAF Alaska):** wire hoops **16–20" (40–50 cm)** high for max strength/wind protection.
- **Taller (WVU, Savvy Gardening):** up to **3–4 ft (90–120 cm)** if the cover stays on longer or for bushier crops. Low tunnels are by definition **≤4 ft tall** (USDA NRCS).
- **Rule of thumb:** keep the apex high enough that **leaves don't press hard against the plastic** (touching plastic burns foliage), low enough to trap heat near the canopy.

### Three caveats for zucchini

1. **Zucchini outgrows a 45 cm tunnel fast.** A mature bush is 60–90 cm tall, 90–120 cm spread, with big fragile leaves that can **break under a floating cover** (UAF). The tunnel is really for the **first ~month of establishment**, then it comes off. UC: "cucurbits like squash need little venting *prior to pollination*."
2. **Pollination.** Bees need access when female flowers open. A closed tunnel at flowering = no fruit. Remove or vent it then.
3. **Heat.** An unvented tunnel can hit **38°C+ (100°F+) inside** on a mild day — you must vent.

### Volcán-specific warning: use it as a rain/pest shelter, NOT a heat trap

Low plastic tunnels are built to **add heat and exclude cold** — but Volcán's problem is **cool + extremely humid**, not cold. In 85–95% RH, a sealed plastic tunnel becomes a **disease chamber**: powdery mildew, downy mildew, bacterial rot, and slugs all thrive (UWyo explicitly warns of this).

**Recommendation for Volcán:**
- Prefer **insect netting / mesh** over sealed plastic — pest exclusion + light rain-break while staying breathable (no humidity trap).
- If you use plastic, do it for **rain exclusion during the wet season** (your real enemy): make it **taller (60–90 cm / 2–3 ft) and aggressively vented** — open ends/sides, anti-drip plastic, drip irrigation underneath, and scout for mildew daily.
- You generally **do not need a tunnel for warmth** — Volcán's 14–24°C is fine for zucchini once established. Tunnels here are for **rain, pest, and seedling protection only**.
