# Cheapest Rain Tunnel and the Limits of Bed Layout (Volcán)

> November 2026. Worldwide review: about 50 searches and full-text reads (Exa + web). It covers the extension services of Japan, Costa Rica, Mexico, Colombia, Taiwan, the Philippines, Laos, Hawaii and the US, plus engineering tests of anchors and plastic film. It builds on [market-garden-space-maximizing-stones-sifting-volcan.md](market-garden-space-maximizing-stones-sifting-volcan.md), [rain-covers-bed-shelters-volcan.md](rain-covers-bed-shelters-volcan.md) and [volcan-tunnel-design.md](volcan-tunnel-design.md).

## Summary

1. **1.2–1.3 m beds with 30 cm paths are already close to the limit.** They put 80–81 % of the land in beds. Wider beds go past arm's reach: you can reach about 40–60 cm comfortably and 55–73 cm with strain (Japanese ergonomic data). Accessibility guides set a 60 cm reach per side. The only gain left is about 2–3 % from narrowing paths to 25 cm inside a tunnel.
2. **The gains that remain come from time, density and height, not layout:**
   - Transplanting instead of direct sowing: +50 % crops per year (Kratky).
   - Denser tomato rows: the top-yielding tunnels in New England harvested 94 % more, with 1.6 ft per drip line against 2.3 ft.
   - Tomato with lettuce underneath: land equivalent ratio (LER) of 1.39, meaning 39 % more harvest than growing them apart.
   - Cucumber on a single vertical string: +20 % over mesh.
   - Dense two-cluster tomatoes: +47 % salable fruit per m² per day under a rain shelter.
3. **Best-value rain cover: yes, arched galvanized tubes over rebar with plastic on top.** It is the standard cheap design everywhere: the Japanese pipe house, the US caterpillar tunnel, the Mexican macrotúnel, and the INTA Costa Rica tunnels.
4. **Recommended design: a walk-in tunnel 3.0 m wide covering two beds.** Two 1.2 m beds, a 30 cm centre path, and about 2.1 m clearance in the middle.
   - Each hoop is two 10 ft (3 m) lengths of ¾″ EMT joined with a coupling, giving a 6 m arch.
   - Plastic comes from a 6 m-wide roll.
   - Hoops sit on ½–⅝″ rebar about 90 cm long, driven 60 cm deep, every 1.2–1.5 m.
   - Ropes go over the plastic between hoops (the "caterpillar" tie-down). The bottom 50–60 cm of each side stays open.
   - Rough cost: about **$6–9/m² covered**, or $8–11/m² of bed. These are estimates; get quotes.
   - Leaf-only beds can use a low single-bed tunnel instead: about 1 m tall, about $6/m².
5. **Volcán timing: the rain and the wind come in different months.** The windiest months (Dec–Mar, 9–12 km/h average, cold-front gusts near 100 km/h) are the dry months, with 4–78 mm of rain a month. **Take the plastic off from about January to March.** That avoids most wind damage and adds about a third to the film's life.

## 1. Bed layout: is there anything better than 1.2–1.3 m beds with 30 cm paths?

| Layout | % of land in beds | Catch |
|---|---|---|
| 0.75 m bed + 0.45 m path (Fortier) | 62.5 % | — |
| 1.2 m + 0.30 m (current plan) | 80 % | — |
| 1.3 m + 0.30 m (Dowding, tunnel) | 81 % | At the reach limit |
| 1.2 m + 0.25 m (tunnel only) | 83 % | Room for feet only, no crates |
| 0.8 m + 0.20 m (MIPS open field) | 80 % | No gain over the current plan |
| 2.33 m + 0.33 m (Paris market gardeners, 1845) | 88 % | Too wide to reach from the paths. They must have stepped onto the beds or used boards (inferred). They also used frames and bell cloches. |
| No paths (block planting) | ~100 % | Compaction. Only for one-pass crops harvested end to end. |

**Reach sets the ceiling.**
- Standing at a raised bed, comfortable reach is 40–60 cm and strained reach is 55–73 cm (Japanese elderly-ergonomics database, quoted by Ohgama et al. 2007).
- Accessibility guides allow at most 60 cm per side, which is 1.2 m for a bed worked from both sides (Vancouver, NYC, Washington State University).
- A 1.3 m bed means 65 cm per side, already a stretch. Anything wider means stepping into the bed, which compacts it.

**Small tweaks that do work**
- **Inside the tunnel:** use 25 cm paths where you never carry anything. Run the outer bed edges right up to the hoop legs, and put short crops in the low zone under the arch.
- **Long beds:** fewer cross lanes means more bed (see the space-maximizing doc).
- **Paths:** sow the edges of the paths, or every second path (Dowding; PimAB), already noted.

**Bigger levers on the same ground** (these add to the earlier doc's list)

| Lever | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Turnover | Transplanting 14-day seedlings after each harvest allows **50 % more crops per year** than direct sowing (Kratky 2006) |
| Density | High-tunnel tomato, top 10 of 48 tunnels: 3.5 vs 1.8 lb/ft² (+94 %), with 1.6 ft per drip line against 2.3 ft and more leaders per area (Grubinger 2024) |
| Short cycles | Dense two-cluster tomatoes: +47 % salable fruit per m² per day under a rain shelter, and half the pesticide per tonne (Kratky 1998) |
| Intercropping | Tomato with lettuce in a tunnel: LER 1.39 (Missouri, Chism) |
| Vertical | Cucumber on a single vertical string: +20 % over mesh trellis (Cornell, Northern New York) |
| Cover itself | Leyte (Philippines), 134 comparisons: higher yields under cover for lettuce, tomato, pepper, chilli, broccoli, cauliflower and beans. No gain for cabbage, cucumber or squash. Whiteflies, aphids and mites were harder to control under cover |

## 2. Which rain cover is the best value?

### The options, cheapest first

| Type | Example | Cost | Good for | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Kit high tunnel (US) | UF/IFAS, 30 × 84 ft | $6.72/ft² (about $72/m²) | — | Far too expensive |

### Why the two-bed arch is the sweet spot for Volcán

- **Everything comes in 6 m.** Two 10 ft (3.05 m) lengths of EMT make a 6.1 m arch. FIDECA in David sells plastic in 6 m × 51 m rolls. Rebar comes in 20 ft (6 m) bars.
- **The geometry works for a 3.0 m span.** Each side has a straight leg of about 0.65 m, topped by a half-circle of 1.5 m radius. These figures are my own arithmetic, not a source design.
  - Centre height: about 2.15 m.
  - Height above a tomato row 45 cm in from the bed's outer edge: about 1.7 m.
  - Height at the bed's outer edge: about 1.3 m, fine for short crops.
  - Two 1.2 m beds plus a 30 cm path fill 80 % of the covered floor, the same share as the open field.
- **A wider tunnel without legs fails.** A 4.3 m ground-to-ground arch over three beds drops to zero height at the outer bed edges. Japan's 5.4 m pipe houses need 22–25 mm pipe every 50 cm.
- **Narrow tunnels stay cooler.** Small rain shelters with open sides stay closer to outside temperature because they have more edge for air to cross (Kratky). Kratky also found rain shelters at 1,200 m in Hawaii ran 10 °C warmer at midday. In Volcán (highs of 22–26 °C) that suits tomatoes. Keep the sides open.

### Recommended build (2 beds × 20 m)

| Part | Spec | Source or reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hoops | 2 × ¾″ EMT (10 ft each) joined with an EMT coupling, bent to the profile above. Use 1″ EMT, or ¾″ galvanized water pipe, on very exposed sites. | Japan: 19–25 mm pipe for 1.2–6 m spans. Cornell: ¾″ galvanized pipe for metal caterpillar tunnels. |
| Bending | Plywood jig with a form radius of about 0.6–0.75× the target. The pipe springs back 1.4–2.3× on bending. Or bend each half as a quarter-arc plus straight leg, then couple the halves at the top. | Grower measurements; Coleman's quarter-circle method |
| Spacing | 1.2–1.5 m on exposed ground, up to 1.8 m if sheltered | Japan: hoops 70 cm apart instead of 50 cm cut strength to about 0.7×. US caterpillar tunnels: 1.5–1.8 m. |
| Anchors | ½–⅝″ rebar 90 cm long, driven 60 cm with 30 cm left above ground. The hoop slides over the rebar, so no pipe touches the soil. | Korean test: 40 cm deep holds 2–3× more than 20 cm. Driving the rebar at about 20° holds 2.5–3.5× more. Holding power rises 1.5–6× after weeks in the ground, so **install before the windy season**. Design value is about 1,000 N per stake. |
| Purlin | Tight rope or EMT along the top. Tie it around every hoop and anchor it to angled stakes about 3 m beyond each end. | Cornell; Kratky (stops water pooling on top) |
| Plastic | UV-stabilized greenhouse film, **180–200 µm, rated 24–36 months, anti-drip**, diffuse if offered. Never construction plastic. | Construction plastic fails in about 6 months (Kratky). Wageningen gives 200 µm a total UV budget of about 350–400 kLy. Volcán's yearly dose is unknown; at an assumed 140–160 kLy that is roughly 2.5 years of exposure. Diffuse film raised tomato yields about 5–15 %. |
| Tie-down | Ropes over the plastic between every pair of hoops, clipped with carabiners to the same rebar. The plastic bunches at each end and is tied to the end stakes. | Cornell; UVM |
| Sides | Bottom 50–60 cm open on both sides all year; ends open | INTA 60 cm side openings; Volcán humidity |
| Orientation | Long axis along the strongest wind (in the dry season the wind is mostly from the north) | INTA Costa Rica; Kentucky. Wind blowing across the tunnel makes the plastic balloon. |
| Protecting the plastic | Paint the tops of the hoops white or wrap them in tape. Avoid sulfur sprays under the plastic. | Hot metal and sulfur or chlorine wear out the film's UV stabilizers (Kratky; Wageningen). |
| Drainage | All roof water lands at the hoop feet, so dig a shallow ditch or mulch the strip | Kratky; Indiana |

- INTA builds them for less with thinner film (0.10–0.15 mm) and bamboo.

### Wind in Volcán: the main risk

- **Volcán is windy.** It is breezier than Boquete, on the windward western slopes. Dry-season cold fronts have brought gusts of about 97 km/h near Volcán Barú and 120 km/h at David airport (1973 account).
- **Plastic-covered houses failed in Hawaii.** In tropical storms, almost all polyethylene-covered houses there were destroyed, while screen-only houses survived (CTAHR). US caterpillar tunnels held at 50 mph (80 km/h) gusts and lost their covers at 65 mph (105 km/h).
- **Rules:**
  1. Have the plastic off from about January to March, the dry, windy months, when cover is least needed.
  2. Make it removable by one person in under 30 minutes: rope tie-down, no glue or batten tape.
  3. Drive the rebar in months before December.
  4. Close-space the hoops (1.2 m) on exposed ground.

**When to use the bigger gable tunnel instead:** for a permanent structure with tall crops grown year-round, or when you want insect-screen walls. That design is in [volcan-tunnel-design.md](volcan-tunnel-design.md). Build the two-bed arch first. It is cheaper, it can be moved each season (rotation), and it tests demand.

## Gaps

- No Panama price was found for greenhouse film, ½″ galvanized pipe or rebar. Get quotes from FIDECA (David), Novey and the Do it Center.
- No study measures how strong a 3 m EMT arch is against wind. The spacing advice is scaled from the Japanese pipe-house tables and US experience.
- The yearly UV dose at Volcán was not found. Film life is estimated from the Wageningen thickness–UV table.
- FIDECA lists 127 and 180 µm film "aditivada 6 y 12 meses" (UV additive for 6 and 12 months). Ask them for a 24–36-month grade. A 12-month film will not last in Volcán's UV.
