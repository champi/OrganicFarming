# Rice Husk in Organic Farming: Best Uses (With or Without Urine), Ranked for Volcán

> **Literature review, Nov 2026.** Every main way rice husk (*cascarilla*, *granza*) is used in organic agriculture worldwide, how well each works, and which ones fit Volcán's Andisol and our inputs (husk, urine, compost, worms, wood ash).
> Companion file: [rice-husk-urine-silica-fertilizer.md](rice-husk-urine-silica-fertilizer.md) covers sealing husk with urine and husk silica chemistry in depth. This file does not repeat it.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Raw husk is a carrier and structure material, not a fertilizer.** It holds little N, P or K (C:N ~50–100), decomposes over years, and ties up nitrogen if dug in raw in large amounts. All of its value comes from what it carries (manure, urine, microbes) or from what it is turned into (char, compost).
2. **Best use overall: carbonize it, charge the char with urine, mix it with compost, and place it in the root zone at a low dose.** In 21 Nepal field trials, urine-charged biochar plus compost out-yielded urine plus compost by +123% on average. An independent Bangladesh trial found +60% on cabbage and kohlrabi. Carbonizing also holds urine N better than raw husk does (19% vs 36% N lost) and makes husk silica more available.
3. **Second best: as the dry, airy part of compost or bokashi.** Mix it with manure or urine so the pile starts at C:N ~20–30. Central American bokashi has always used it (about 1 part in 4–6).
4. **Third: as mulch.** A 5–10 cm layer cut weeds by 77–91% and kept soil wetter and cooler. Mulched cucumber yielded +78%.
5. **Also good:** carbonized husk at 25–40% of a seedling mix, and raw husk at 10–40% of worm bedding.
6. **Weak uses:** rice husk ash as a potassium source (only ~0.5% K₂O; wood ash is far richer), and burying lots of raw husk just before planting (it starves crops of nitrogen).
7. **Volcán caveats:** Andosols already have fairly high available silicon, so the silicon benefit may be smaller than in rice-paddy studies. Husk silicon is also unlikely to free much phosphorus from Andisol allophane.

## 2. Ranking for Volcán

| Rank | Use | Evidence strength | Main gain | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Carbonized husk charged with urine, mixed with compost, placed in the root zone | Strong (many field trials) | Yield, N retention, CEC, Si | Labour to carbonize; burning to ash wastes it |
| 2 | Co-compost or bokashi ingredient (with manure or urine) | Strong | Structure, bulk, Si-rich humus | Needs an N source and turning |

## 3. Carbonized husk (biochar, *cascarilla carbonizada*, *kuntan*)

### 3.1 Why carbonize

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Rice husk biochar raises soil organic C, CEC, available K and microbial activity, and cuts nitrate leaching | Asadi et al. 2021 (review, *Rice Science*) |
| Plant-available Si (CaCl₂-extractable) rises up to ~3× over raw husk. Best: ≤1 mm husk at 500 °C (2,562 vs 790 mg/kg raw). Above 600–700 °C silica starts to crystallize and availability falls | Olanrewaju et al. 2023 |
| At 300 °C, available Si is *lower* than raw husk because the organic matrix still covers the silica | Olanrewaju et al. 2023 |
| **Disagreement:** in longer dissolution tests, uncharred husk released Si faster than char, and short lab tests overstate char's Si release | Adamescu et al. 2021 |
| Composting-toilet test: husk char lost 19% of N vs 36% for raw husk, and sent 12.8% of its N into plants (the highest of four cover materials) | Composting-toilet matrix study (biochar vs sawdust, husk, corn stalk) |
| 5–10% husk char added to chicken-manure compost sped composting and cut early N loss from 10.3% to ~0.4%. Cucumber yield: 51 t/ha (10% char compost) vs lowest yield without char | Manickam 2013 (UPM thesis) |

**Practical temperature:** 450–550 °C with complete black char: no yellow (raw) and no grey (ash). On a farm this means a simple chimney carbonizer: a 20 L can or a drum with a chimney, husk piled around it, stirred, and quenched with water when it is black (PhilRice "Farm carbonisation machine"). Grey or white ash means it ran too hot.

### 3.2 Charging with urine: the step that makes the yield difference

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Pumpkin, 8 farms: urine-soaked biochar (1:1 by volume) + compost = 82.6 t/ha. That is +306% vs urine + compost and +85% vs biochar + compost, at only 0.75 t/ha char in the planting pit | Schmidt et al. 2015 (*Agriculture*) |
| 21 trials, 13 crops: urine-charged char + compost gave **+123% ± 77%** vs the farmer practice of urine + compost (13 trials). It beat NPK-charged char by +103%. Gains per crop ranged from +15 to +298% | Schmidt et al. 2017 (*Land Degrad. Dev.*) |
| Bangladesh smallholders: 1:1 urine-char, ~2 L/m² (~2 t char/ha). Cabbage and kohlrabi +60% per area, with heads ~50% heavier | Sutradhar et al. 2021 (*Environ. Dev. Sustain.*) |
| Bangladesh organic rice: biochar-compost + cow urine was the best treatment (4.03 t/ha) | Tahura et al. 2025 (*Sci. Rep.*) |
| Earlier on-farm Nepal results: pumpkin +364%, tomato +21%, onion +18% | Farming First 2016 |

**Agreement:** charging plus root-zone placement matter more than the dose. Most trials used 0.5–2 t/ha in pits or furrows rather than broadcasting.
**Caveat:** the largest effects come from one research group (Ithaka/Schmidt). Independent trials (Bangladesh, rice) show smaller but still clear gains of ~20–60%.

### 3.3 Results on Andosols specifically

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Andosol paddy, Japan: husk charcoal at 10–40 t/ha did not change first-year rice yield. Soil C rose by 98–149% of the char C added (vs 41% for raw husk). Raw husk at 20 t/ha raised CH₄ by 227% | Koyama et al. 2016 (*Soil Sci. Plant Nutr.*) |
| 3 years at 20 t/ha: rice took up more Si and grew more straw (+14%), but grain yield did not rise. Over 72% of the char C stayed in the soil | Koyama & Hayashi 2017 (*Plant Prod. Sci.*) |
| Repeated char lowered 1000-grain weight, probably because the high C:N ties up N late in the season. The authors advise an N top-dress | Koyama & Hayashi 2019 |

**Reading for Volcán:** on an already fertile, high-organic-matter Andisol, bulk char alone does little for yield. The gains come from *charged* char placed at the root, with an N source alongside. This matches the Nepal results.

## 4. Compost and bokashi

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Chicken manure + crushed husk composts best at a starting C:N of 20 (over 50 °C for 7 days, germination index 91%, 60% moisture) | Gu 2015; Chinese thesis 2016 |
| Ghana, by volume: husk : chicken droppings 2:2 gave 2.26% N and C:N < 20, the best of the ratios tested | Murimi & Gbedemah 2023 |
| Husk + chicken slurry + feed + molasses (20:1:1:1), 60 days: CEC ~101 cmol/kg, and the compost cut NH₃ loss from urea | Omar et al. 2021 (*Sustainability*) |
| Adding cow dung to husk compost raised the temperature (52.8 → 60.1 °C) and cut N loss. Husk composts germinated Solanaceae at 93–95% vs 81% in a peat substrate | Zhang et al. 2025 (PMC12623384) |
| Human urine as the N source composted faster than urea (earlier 65 °C peak, 22% more breakdown, 45% more mineral N after 84 days) | Fatunbi & Mnkeni 2009 |
| Weekly cow urine on a manure + straw pile raised N 1.5× and P 2× and shortened composting | Nguyen & Nguyen 2018 |
| Costa Rica bokashi: husk (*granza*) is the aeration ingredient, e.g. 10 sacks husk : 10 chicken manure : 10 soil : 30 other manures/coffee pulp, plus charcoal, molasses, yeast and lime. Ready in 2–4 weeks | Garita, PLATICAR (MAG Costa Rica) |
| Classic Latin American recipe: 1 sack charcoal : 1 chicken manure : 1 husk : 1 rice bran : 3 soil + molasses. Dose 10–30 g per leafy plant, up to 100–120 g per tomato or pepper | Ortega 2012 (U. Cuenca), after Soto 2004 |
| Bokashi in Chiapas (pepper, onion): onion yield rose from 6.4 to 21 t/ha | Álvarez-Solís et al. 2016 |

**Using urine in the pile:** urine can replace manure as the N source. Pour stored urine onto the husk layers while building the pile until it reaches the "squeeze" moisture (a few drops between the fingers), then turn it. That is the aerobic version of what our sealed buckets cannot do (see the companion file).

## 5. Mulch

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Tanzania (*Brassica carinata*): husk mulch at 5 cm cut weeds by 77% and at 10 cm by 91%. 5 cm gave the best yield (+47% vs unweeded, similar to hand-weeding) | Amiri et al. 2025 |
| Indonesia (cucumber, Ultisol): husk mulch cut weeds by 58% and raised soil moisture from 28.8 to 35.2%. Mulches overall raised marketable yield by +78% | Bengkulu trial 2018 (*J. Appl. Hortic.*) |
| Nigeria (cucumber): husk mulch gave the coolest soil (27.4 vs 28.1 °C bare) and the heaviest fruit | IOSR-JAVS cucumber trial |
| Nigeria (tomato): any mulch at 15 t/ha gave 23.3 vs 8.1 t/ha unmulched. Mulch type made no difference | Tswanya 2017 |
| Nepal (cucumber): husk mulch let the most weeds through of the mulches tested. Plastic was best | Karki et al. 2020 |
| Container nurseries: parboiled hulls at ≥1–2.5 cm gave full weed and liverwort control. Steaming kills seed; raw hulls are unsterilized | USDA-ARS (Altland); NC State Extension |

**Reading:** husk mulch works when it is thick (≥5 cm), and it keeps soil cooler, which suits cilantro and lettuce. It blows away in wind, and on Volcán slopes heavy rain can wash it off, so tuck it under a coarser mulch or use it in beds with edges. Raw husk can sprout rice and carry weed seed, so compost or char it first if that matters. N tie-up stays small because the husk sits on the surface, not mixed into the root zone.

## 6. Seedling and potting mixes

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Tomato seedlings: the best quality came from **24–38% carbonized husk** across four substrates | da Silva et al. 2012 |
| Lettuce seedlings: leaf area fell steadily as carbonized husk rose from 0 to 100% | Freitas et al. 2013 |
| Ground raw husk (≤2 mm) at 60–90% with peat grew tomato seedlings as well as peat, with N fed by fertigation | Nutt & Evans 2005 (U. Arkansas) |
| Rwanda: sand : goat manure : carbonized husk 50:10:40 gave tomato seedlings close to peat. They went on to yield 92 t/ha | Nkurunziza et al. 2022 |
| Pure raw husk: 0–20% germination. Burnt husk mixed with soil + manure gave the best seedlings | Eviness & Nyirenda 2025 |
| Costa Rica: 50% vermicompost + 25% bokashi + 25% husk beat peat moss for tomato transplants | Garbanzo & Navarro 2015 |

**Reading:** use carbonized husk at **~25–35%** in place of perlite, never as the main ingredient. Check pH, since fresh char is ~8.5–9.

## 7. Worm bedding

Cow manure with **10–40% husk** produced more young *Eisenia fetida* (824–844 vs 769) and heavier young at 40%. Casting output fell as the husk share rose (Korea, Daegu Univ. 2006). A feed C:N of ~25 gave the most worm biomass (Mekelle Univ. thesis). Use husk as the dry bulking part, not the food.

## 8. Rice husk ash

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Bangladesh maize, 3 years: husk ash (22–27% Si) at 2.5 t/ha raised grain yield by 17–19% | Haque et al. 2026 |
| Nigeria rice: husk ash and husk dust at 2.5–12.5 t/ha (+ poultry manure) roughly doubled grain yield vs control and raised soil Si and P | Nwite et al. 2019 |
| Potato in cocopeat: 20 g ash per pot gave +17.7% tubers and delayed senescence under drought | Sharma et al. (ICAR-CPRI) |
| Husk ash contains only **~0.5–0.6% K₂O**, 0.6% P₂O₅ and 0.1% N | Myanmar study; ICAR-CPRI analysis |
| Burning above ~700 °C (and fully to ash) crystallizes silica and lowers its solubility | Olanrewaju 2023; Linam et al. 2022 |

**Reading:** ash is a silicon and liming material, not a potassium fertilizer. For K, wood ash or SOP is far better. Grasses (maize, rice) respond to Si the most.

## 9. Silicon on Volcán's Andisol: what to expect

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| A survey of 180 Japanese fields found Andosols relatively *high* in available Si, and upland soils higher than paddies | Yanai et al. 2016 (*Soil Sci. Plant Nutr.*) |
| Meta-analysis of 15 studies: Si fertilizer raised crop P uptake by +52% on average | Mückschel et al. 2025 (*Plant Soil*) |
| Si competes well with P for sorption sites only at **pH > 6–7**. At pH 5 there was no effect | Lee & Kim 2007; Haynes & Zhou 2018 |
| On Andisol amorphous minerals, which adsorbed over 98% of the P, silicic acid at 100 mg/L **did not usefully reduce P adsorption** | Hanudin et al. 2014 |
| Soil Si reduced powdery mildew on pumpkin and made fungicides work better. Husk and straw composts are cited as Si sources | Rutgers (Heckman); wollastonite pumpkin trial (*HortTechnology* 2019) |
| Si did not limit downy mildew in organic cucumber, but it cut anthracnose | SARE LS06-187 (Florida) |

**Reading:** expect modest Si gains on Volcán's Andisol. The best case is cucurbits (powdery mildew) and grasses (corn). Don't count on husk silicon to unlock phosphorus there; keep relying on bone ash and mycorrhizal fungi for P.

## 10. With urine: what works best

1. **Best:** char the husk, soak it 1:1 by volume in stored urine for a few days, mix it with compost, and put it in the planting hole or furrow (§3.2).
2. **Good:** use urine as the N source of a turned husk compost or bokashi pile (§4).
3. **OK:** raw husk soaked in urine (our sealed buckets) is mainly ammonium-charged husk. Use it as the N part of a compost pile, or dig it into a trench 2–3 weeks before planting. Raw husk holds urine N less well than char (36% vs 19% N lost in the toilet study).
4. **As cover material:** husk or husk char covers human waste in dry toilets. Char gives the best N retention and decomposition. The extra ammonia from urine also helps kill pathogens (Global Water Pathogen Project).

**Safety (WHO 2006, SSWM):** store urine ≥1 month for household use. Stop applying it about 1 month before harvesting crops that are eaten raw.

---

## 11. Contaminants

- Husk biochar *lowered* cadmium in crops: wheat grain Cd −31 to −39% (2022 study, PMC9763249), rice grain Cd −13 to −25% (meta-analysis).
- No clear arsenic benefit (Linam et al. 2022).
- None of the sources reported As or Cd levels in the husk itself. Husk from a large commercial mill is generally low-risk, but this gap remains.

---

## 12. Gaps

- No trial was found of husk products on tropical highland Andisols growing vegetables.
- There is no head-to-head field test of raw husk + urine vs husk char + urine. The toilet study (N loss) is the closest.
- No price was found for husk from mills in Chiriquí. The Agrosilos mill in David burns 40% of its husk for power, so ask mills directly.
