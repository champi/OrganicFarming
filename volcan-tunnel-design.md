# Volcán Rain Shelter Design — Research Synthesis

> **August 2026. Compiled from extensive worldwide research on protected cultivation structures for tropical highland climates (1,200–1,400m elevation, 13–22°C, 2,400–4,400mm annual rainfall, 74–90% humidity).**

---

## 1. Volcán Climate Profile — Why Standard Advice Doesn't Apply

| Factor | Volcán, Chiriquí | Northern Temperate (Maine, Europe) |
|---|---|---|
| **Elevation** | 1,200–1,500 m | Sea level–500 m |
| **Temperature** | 13–22°C year-round | -20 to +35°C seasonal |
| **Annual rainfall** | 2,400–4,400 mm | 800–1,200 mm |
| **Peak month rain** | October: 630 mm | July: 100 mm |
| **Humidity** | 74–90% | 50–70% |
| **Snow** | None | Yes |
| **Climate classification** | Cfb (oceanic highland) / Tropical monsoon | Dfb (humid continental) |

**Fundamental design inversion:** Northern greenhouses trap heat. Volcán needs to **shed heat and humidity while blocking rain**. The design priorities are reversed.

---

## 2. Why the Eliot Coleman Quonset Design Is Wrong for Volcán

The Coleman Modular Greenhouse (1" EMT Quonset, sealed plastic, no ridge vent) was designed for **Maine winters**. Each feature works against Volcán's needs:

| Coleman Feature | Problem in Volcán | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Quonset shape** (half-round) | Warm air pools against flat ceiling. No chimney effect. | CFD models: Gothic achieves 30% higher ventilation rates than Quonset. |
| **Sealed plastic envelope** | 90% humidity + greenhouse effect = fungal breeding chamber | Malaysia MARDI: tunnel-roof reached +6.57°C above ambient |
| **No ridge vent** | Hot humid air has no escape path | Colombia study: without roof vents, thermal gradients of 7.6–12.2°C |
| **Plastic edges buried in soil** | 600mm rain/month floods the buried edges, channels water in | IRAT Martinique: explicitly rejected buried-plastic tunnels for tropical rain |
| **Designed to trap heat** | Volcán needs to shed heat | All tropical research: target <2°C differential inside vs outside |

## 3. The Worldwide Evidence: What Actually Works

### 3.1 Malaysia (MARDI) — Direct Shape Comparison

The only head-to-head measurement of tunnel vs jack-roof in tropical conditions:

| Metric | Tunnel-Roof (side vents only) | Jack-Roof (side + roof vents) | Outside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Max temperature excess | **+6.57°C** | **+3.35°C** | — |
| Min relative humidity | 41.10% | 51.13% | 56.98% |

**Conclusion:** Adding a roof vent cut temperature excess by 50%. Ridge ventilation is non-negotiable.

_Source: MARDI, ISHS Acta Horticulturae 710.17 (2006)_

### 3.2 Sri Lanka — A-Frame vs Curved Roof

Tested at 750m elevation in Kandy (similar rainfall to Volcán: 2,000–2,500mm/year):

> "The A-frame (slanted roof) was comparatively advantageous over the conventional arch frame (curved roof) for keeping daytime temperature lower."

With ridge vent added, further reduction in temperature and humidity. Minimum vent ratio: 4.7% of floor area. Recommended: ≥30%.

_Source: Weerakkody et al., Int. J. Biol. Chem. Sci. 3(6), 2009_

### 3.3 Martinique (IRAT/CIRAD) — The Chimney-Effect Tunnel

French Caribbean, nearly identical rainfall to Volcán (2,000mm/year), similar humidity (81%):

> "Classic tunnels (plastic cover buried on the sides, without gable ends) were inadequate. The inside diurnal temperatures raised too high for most vegetables."

**Their solution:** A chimney-effect tunnel with:
- Continuous low openings (side vents)
- Continuous top opening (ridge vent)
- Transparent plastic gutter under the ridge opening — collects rain while letting hot air escape
- Static natural ventilation only (no fans, no electricity)

> "The objective is to keep the temperature as close as possible inside and outside. With heat-tolerant cultivars it is difficult to obtain quality tomato fruit with long exposures to temperatures higher than 30°C."

_Source: Rault, Acta Horticulturae 281, 1990_

### 3.4 Benguet, Philippines — Double Cladding for Tropical Highlands

**Most relevant to Volcán.** Benguet (~1,500m elevation, cool highland, heavy rain, typhoons). BSU research by Dr. John Malamug:

| Crop | Best Cladding | Yield Increase |
|---|---|---|
| Romaine lettuce | Clear plastic + **40% shade net** | +22.3% |
| Broccoli | Clear plastic + **40% shade net** | +46.9% |
| Strawberry | Clear plastic + **50% shade net** | +20.8% |

**Design:** Metal arch frame with two layers — clear plastic underneath (rain block), shade net on top (heat/radiation reduction). Mesh sides for ventilation.

_Source: DOST-PCAARRD, Philippines, 2018_

### 3.5 Colombia (Villagrán et al.) — Hillside Ventilation Failure

Andean region, studied roof structures on sloped terrain:

> "Ventilation rates were far below the minimum recommended value (0.04 m³/s/m²) due to the unavailability of ventilation areas in the roof area. Thermal gradients between 7.6 and 12.2°C."

**Key finding:** No roof vents = 12°C temperature variation inside a single structure. Side + roof ventilation increased air exchange by 144–449% over either alone. Taller structures (increased gutter height) reduced temperature by up to 11.7°C.

_Source: Villagrán et al., multiple papers 2019–2021_

### 3.6 DutchGreenhouses — High-Altitude Tropical Zone Definition

For commercial greenhouses in Kenyan highlands, Ethiopian highlands, Colombian Andes, Ecuadorian Andes:

> "Sit on the equator and climb to two thousand metres and you get an unusual combination: intense, consistent radiation with genuinely cool nights. A greenhouse here is not a simple structure. It is a light and temperature buffer working hard in both directions on the same day."

Requirements: height and vent area for midday heat, screens for night retention, UV-rated covering.

_Source: DutchGreenhouses.com/climate-zones/high-altitude-tropical_

### 3.7 FAO Guidelines — Tropical Highlands vs Lowlands

> "If the mean minimum outside temperature is lower than 12°C, lockable ventilation openings have to be installed. With mean minimum outside temperatures above 12°C, ventilation openings can be permanently open."

**For Volcán:** Mean minimum ~13°C. Borderline — mostly open ventilation, with ability to close during coolest nights (Jan–Feb lows ~13°C). Nets on ventilation openings reduce efficiency remarkably — must be accounted for in vent sizing.

_Source: FAO, ISHS Acta Horticulturae 578, 2002_

### 3.8 Wageningen UR + Indonesia — CFD-Optimized Tropical Greenhouse

Wageningen University used computational fluid dynamics to design for Indonesian conditions:

- 200µm UV-blocking polyethylene (3-year tropical lifespan)
- NIR-reflecting film to reduce heat load
- High light diffusion properties
- Insect net on all ventilation openings
- Optimized vent size and position by simulation

_Source: Hemming et al., Acta Horticulturae 710, 2006_

### 3.9 Kenya (Grekkon Limited) — Commercial Highland Practice

Kenyan greenhouse company serving equatorial highlands:

> "Tunnel type units are for the highlands and cooler areas. Open ventilation type units for the warm mid to coastal locations."

For highland tunnels: UV-treated anti-drip polythene (prevents dew formation — critical for fungal disease prevention), insect net sides of varying shade density depending on crop.

_Source: Grekkon Limited, Kenya, 2025_

### 3.10 Hainan, China — Tropical CFD Optimization

145m × 432m plastic greenhouse with semi-open roof. Field measurements + CFD simulation:

- Average indoor temp 24–26°C (tropical conditions)
- Humidity 76–87% annually
- Combined side + roof ventilation: average airflow 0.6 m/s
- Key optimization: increasing side window area improved roof vent utilization

> "Optimizing the size and configuration of vents can promote roof ventilation efficiency. Design greenhouse orientation according to maximum air volume in summer."

_Source: MDPI Agronomy 14(4), 2024_

## 4. The Panama Context — What's Been Built Where

### 4.1 IDIAP/FONTAGRO Prototypes — NOT for Volcán

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | El Ejido, **Los Santos** (Azueros peninsula) |
| Elevation | ~25m (sea level) |
| Climate | Hot/dry tropical lowland |
| Design goal | Maximum heat shedding for "zonas de alta temperatura" |
| Weather data source | ETESA station La Villa de Los Santos (45 years) |
| Prototype type | Multi-capilla (multi-gable) with dual zenithal openings at 8.5–9.5m |
| Key result | Internal temperature similar to outside, sometimes cooler during wind |

**Important:** This was designed for sea-level heat. The thermal requirements are fundamentally different from Volcán's cool highland. The **shape principles** (gable, ridge vents, mesh sides) transfer, but the **thermal design** does not.

### 4.2 UTP Modular Design — General Tropical

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Design type | Even-span gable (capilla a dos aguas), modular |
| Dimensions | 3m to gutter, 3.99m to ridge, 3m × 3m base |
| Covering | LDPE plastic roof + anti-aphid mesh walls |
| Anchoring | Helicoidal ground anchors (pilotes helicoidales) |
| Construction | Telescopic columns, bolted connections |

**Important:** This was a university design concept for "clima tropical de Panamá" generally — not field-tested at a specific elevation. Shape principles transfer; thermal specifics may not.

### 4.3 De Boquete Greenhouse — The Actual Highland Installation

## 5. Recommended Design for Volcán

### 5.1 Shape: Even-Span Gable with Continuous Ridge Vent

```
          Shade net (40-50%)
         /=================\
        //                 \\      ← Clear UV poly roof (6-mil)
       //   RIDGE VENT     \\     ← 15-20cm gap at peak
      //    (with gutter)   \\    ← Transparent gutter below to catch rain
     //                     \\
    |                       |
    |   INSECT MESH (40)    |      ← Permanent side ventilation
    |                       |
    |_______________________|
         Raised beds
      Perimeter gravel drain
```

### 5.2 Why This Shape

| Feature | Justification | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Gable roof (≥30° pitch)** | Channels hot air to ridge via chimney effect | Sri Lanka: A-frame outperformed curved roof |
| **Continuous ridge vent** | Non-negotiable for tropical humidity control | Malaysia: +6.6°C vs +3.4°C excess |
| **Gutter under ridge vent** | Collects rain entering through vent without blocking airflow | IRAT Martinique design |
| **Double cladding** (plastic + shade net) | Blocks rain + reduces radiation heat load | Benguet: +22-47% yield over single layer |
| **Permanent mesh sidewalls** (40 mesh) | Continuous passive ventilation at 74-90% humidity | FONTAGRO manual, Wageningen, IDIAP |
| **Height ≥3m at gutter** | Taller = stronger chimney effect | Colombia: 0.1-11.7°C reduction from added height |
| **Orientation perpendicular to prevailing wind** | Maximizes cross-ventilation through mesh | Hainan CFD study, all tropical research |
| **No buried plastic edges** | Prevents flooding in heavy rain | IRAT explicitly rejected burial |
| **Plastic overhang + sandbags** | Secures edges without creating water channels | Cornell QuickHoops manual |

### 5.3 Dimensions

| Parameter | Recommendation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Module size | 3m × 3m or 3m × 6m | EMT comes in 3m (10') lengths |
| Gutter height | 2.5–3.0m | For working comfort + chimney effect |
| Ridge height | 3.5–4.0m | ≥1m above gutter for steep pitch |
| Roof pitch | ≥30° (5.8:12 or steeper) | Channels heat, sheds 600mm/month rain |
| Ridge vent width | 15–20cm continuous gap | ≥4.7% of floor area minimum (Sri Lanka study) |
| Vent ratio (total) | ≥40% of floor area | FONTAGRO manual standard |

### 5.4 Materials

| Component | Material | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | 1" EMT (electrical conduit) | Available at Novey. Straight cuts, no bending needed for gable. |
| **Connectors** | EMT corner/ridge connectors + self-tapping screws | Bolted assembly, no welding |
| **Roof plastic** | 6-mil UV-stabilized greenhouse polyethylene | Anti-drip properties critical for humidity |
| **Shade net** | 40% for leafy crops, 50% for fruiting crops | Benguet research validated |
| **Side mesh** | 40-mesh anti-aphid insect net, white/pearl, ≤30% shade | Standard tropical spec (FONTAGRO) |
| **Anchor** | Helicoidal ground anchors or driven EMT sleeves with concrete | UTP recommendation for Panama soils |
| **Gutters** | PVC pipe split lengthwise or commercial gutter | 600mm/month rain requires serious drainage |
| **Corrosion protection** | Cold-galvanizing spray on all cut ends, scratches, screw holes | 90% humidity will find every bare metal spot |
| **Plastic-to-metal insulation** | Split foam pipe insulation at all contact points | Prevents heat degradation of PE film |

### 5.5 Critical Construction Details

**Drainage (priority #1 for Volcán):**
- Perimeter trench: 20–30cm deep, filled with gravel, sloped away from structure
- Roof overhang: 30–50cm beyond walls so rain falls into trench
- Raised beds inside: 15–20cm above surrounding ground level
- Gutters on both eaves draining to storage or away from beds
- Site on slight slope if possible (1–2% grade)

**Ventilation (priority #2):**
- Ridge vent must be continuous along entire length — not just end vents
- Side mesh from ground to 2m height minimum on all sides
- Option: roll-up plastic panels over mesh for cold nights (Jan–Feb lows ~13°C)
- Ridge vent rain cap: second plastic sheet suspended 15cm above gap

**Anchoring (priority #3):**
- Helicoidal anchors at corners (UTP recommendation)
- Intermediate posts driven 30cm into ground
- Volcanic soil is well-drained but can be rocky — pre-drill with bar if needed
- End-wall diagonal bracing or guy-lines to stakes at 45° angle

**Anti-corrosion (priority #4):**
- Cold-galv spray every cut end of EMT immediately after cutting
- Paint or spray all self-tapping screw penetrations
- Foam wrap at every plastic-to-metal contact point
- Inspect and reapply annually before wet season

## 6. What NOT to Build in Volcán

| Design | Problem |
|---|---|
| **Sealed Quonset/Coleman tunnel** | Traps heat (+8-10°C), traps humidity, no ridge vent |
| **Plastic buried on sides** | Floods in heavy rain, channels water into structure |
| **No ridge vent** | Condensation drips on plants, humidity builds, temperature gradients of 7-12°C |
| **Single-layer plastic only** | Overheating from direct radiation. Shade net layer is proven to increase yields 22-47% |
| **PVC hoops** | Degrade in tropical UV, collapse under heavy rain, documented failures |
| **Wood frame without treatment** | Rots in 90% humidity within 2-3 years |
| **Greenhouse designed for cold climates** | Fundamentally wrong thermal model. "Invernadero no aplica en regiones cálidas del trópico" |
| **Insufficient height** (<2m gutter) | No chimney effect, poor ventilation, uncomfortable to work in |

## 7. Crop-Specific Recommendations

Based on the Benguet research and your OrganicFarming intent graph:

| Crop | Plastic | Shade Net % | Special Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lettuce / leafy greens** | Clear UV poly | 40% | Keep temp <25°C. Double cladding critical. |
| **Brassicas** (broccoli, etc.) | Clear UV poly | 40% | Same as lettuce. Benguet: +46.9% yield. |
| **Tomatoes** (cherry) | Clear UV poly | 40% | Need trellis support. Pollen sterile >35°C. |
| **Peppers** | Clear UV poly | 40% | Heat-tolerant but need ventilation for fruit set. |
| **Strawberries** | Clear UV poly | 50% | More shade needed. Benguet: +20.8% yield. |
| **Herbs** (basil, cilantro, parsley) | Clear UV poly | 40% | Good air circulation prevents fungal issues. |

**Dry season (Jan–Mar):** Shade net may need to be removed or reduced to 30% during lower-light months.

**Wet season (May–Nov):** Full double cladding + ensure ridge vent is fully open. Monitor for condensation.
